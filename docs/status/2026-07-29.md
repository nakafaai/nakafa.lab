# Daily Research Journal: 2026-07-29

Audience: founder / researcher
Status: matched-sibling HSCT held; long-term safety comparator added

## Question And Decision

**Question:** Does a newly indexed long-term HSCT meta-analysis close the
durability and late-safety gaps enough to promote matched-sibling HSCT beyond
the leading affordable-curative route benchmark?

**Decision:** `hold_msd_hsct_at_leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

The late-indexed evidence is material because it adds 15- to 39-year survival
and late-effect data. It does not support broader promotion because the
synthesis mixes time horizons, contains denominator conflicts, appears to
include overlapping Pescara cohorts, and has no modern LMIC or Indonesia
delivery evidence.

**Confidence:** High that promotion is unsupported. Low that the pooled 85%
estimate predicts a contemporary LMIC or Indonesia route.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact and meta-analysis:** PMID `42520329` reviewed four observational
  reports and states that 616 transplanted participants contributed 15-, 20-,
  30-, or 39-year overall-survival estimates. The pooled estimate was 85%
  (95% CI 81%-89%; `I2=40%`). Its literature search ended on 2024-07-10.
- **Fact and primary cohort:** The most detailed included report, PMID
  `36002533`, followed 137 consecutive people transplanted at one Pescara
  center from 1983 through 2018. The median age at transplant was 10.1 years,
  and 127 received a genotypically HLA-identical sibling graft.
- **Fact and durable outcome:** At final follow-up, 114 of 137 were living and
  108 were living and cured. Thirty-nine-year overall survival was 81.4% and
  disease-free survival was 74.5%. The report recorded 13 early
  transplant-related deaths, one late transplant-related death, and 12
  thalassemia recurrences.
- **Fact and late effects:** Fifteen secondary solid cancers were reported,
  with a 39-year cumulative incidence of 16.4%. Azoospermia was reported in 22
  of 42 evaluated males, and primary or secondary amenorrhea in 32 of 62
  evaluated females. These are observed cohort outcomes, not proof that one
  conditioning component caused every event.
- **Evidence tier and scope:** The synthesis is peer-reviewed secondary
  evidence from observational cohorts. The Pescara report is direct primary
  human evidence, but 112 of 137 participants were transplanted in the 1980s or
  1990s. Neither source evaluates the modern 2026 LMIC protocol.

## Contradictions And Cohort Independence

- **Count conflict:** The meta-analysis reports 616 total participants, but its
  four listed study sizes sum to 618.
- **Donor conflict:** The narrative states that 522 participants received
  matched-sibling grafts and 96 received unrelated grafts, totaling 618. The
  study table instead sums to 504 matched siblings, 95 unrelated donors, and 19
  other related donors.
- **Probable overlap:** The included 2008 report covers 115 Pescara recipients
  transplanted from 1983 through 2006. The included 2022 report covers 137
  Pescara recipients transplanted from 1983 through 2018, shares senior authors,
  and cites the earlier report. The earlier cohort appears nested in the later
  cohort, but the meta-analysis does not report how participant overlap was
  handled.
- **Interpretation:** The pooled denominator cannot be treated as 616 or 618
  confirmed independent people. Pooling estimates from four different time
  horizons also does not create a survival probability at one common time
  point.

## Cure-Program Implications

- **Mechanism:** Matched-sibling HSCT can replace ineffective recipient
  hematopoiesis with donor hematopoiesis after conditioning. The primary cohort
  shows that durable cure is possible, while also making late recurrence,
  mortality, endocrine, fertility, and secondary-cancer surveillance explicit.
- **Ranking:** Matched-sibling HSCT remains above individualized ex vivo
  gene-cell therapy on current delivered-cost evidence, but only as a benchmark
  for the narrow pediatric donor and risk scope.
- **Affordability and access:** Neither long-term source reports itemized
  contemporary delivered cost, Indonesia capacity, authorization, payer
  coverage, access, or affordability. Lifetime cancer, endocrine, fertility,
  recurrence, and organ surveillance remain outside the reported
  USD 8,000-10,000 LMIC route.
- **Hypothesis:** An independently enrolled, contemporary matched-sibling
  pediatric cohort using one protocol and one time-specific endpoint can
  preserve five-year thalassemia-free survival while reducing the historical
  recurrence, mortality, endocrine, fertility, and secondary-cancer burden.
- **Open question:** What complete five-year efficacy and late-safety
  denominators accompany the modern LMIC protocol, and what does lifetime
  monitoring add to Indonesia-relevant delivered cost?
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove the leading-route label if independent
  modern follow-up shows material loss of thalassemia-free survival, excess
  death, recurrence, graft failure, infection, GVHD, endocrine or fertility
  harm, or secondary malignancy, or if the itemized Indonesia-relevant route
  exceeds the program's delivered-cost threshold.
- **Next decisive action:** Reconcile study counts and Pescara participant
  overlap before using the pooled estimate. Require one independent,
  contemporary five-year matched-sibling cohort with a time-specific
  thalassemia-free-survival endpoint, complete late-safety denominators, and an
  itemized lifetime Indonesia delivery model.
