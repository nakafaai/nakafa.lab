# Research Status: 2026-08-20

Audience: general research planning
Status: third YOLT-204 registry record integrated and held at pre-results benchmark only

## Question

Does the newly posted YOLT-204 record establish an active, TDT-defined systemic
genetic-intervention route strongly enough to change the affordable-cure ranking?

## Decision

`hold_yolt_204_at_three_record_pre_results_systemic_genetic_intervention_benchmark_only_pending_protocol_identity_and_first_dosed_results`

The three YOLT-204 records remain separate registry signals and their planned
enrollment must not be summed. CS-101 remains the stronger reported Asia editing
benchmark. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## What Changed

ClinicalTrials.gov posted [`NCT07774351`](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07774351)
on August 19. It is a third YOLT-204 record, distinct from the TDT-specific
[`NCT06678165`](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06678165) and the pediatric
mixed-hemoglobinopathy [`NCT07190001`](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07190001).
The new record strengthens the traceability of the planned systemic genetic
intervention because it identifies four intravenous dose groups, classifies
YOLT-204 as a genetic intervention, and measures intentionally modified alleles
in peripheral-blood leukocytes and bone-marrow cells.

It does not supply a treated participant, posted result, TDT-specific enrollment
denominator, molecular editor, genomic target, complete delivery formulation,
off-target or clonal-safety endpoint, delivered cost, payer path, or Indonesia
access result. The route therefore stays on hold at a registry benchmark only.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - design and population:** `NCT07774351` is a not-yet-recruiting,
  single-arm, open-label, nonrandomized dose-escalation record planning up to 30
  people aged 14 to 35 with TDT or SCD. It estimates a September 30, 2026 start
  and April 1, 2028 completion. No results are posted.
- **Fact - intervention and endpoints:** The registry classifies YOLT-204 as a
  genetic intervention with planned doses of 0.6, 1.0, 1.25, and an unresolved
  mg/kg dose. Primary outcomes are adverse events, sustained transfusion
  reduction for at least three months, and HbF of at least 20% for at least
  three months without hydroxyurea, all through week 52. Secondary outcomes
  include intentionally modified alleles in blood and marrow, total hemoglobin,
  HbF, and F-cell proportion.
- **Fact - three-record identity:** The records have different responsible
  institutions and protocol IDs: `YOLT-204-IIT`, `YOLT-204-IIT-002`, and
  `YOLT-204-IIT-005`. They plan 3 adults, 18 children, and 30 adolescents or
  adults, respectively. ClinicalTrials.gov exposes no official duplicate,
  replacement, or extension relation among them.
- **Fact - current execution state:** All three records remain not yet
  recruiting and have no posted results. `NCT06678165` still shows an estimated
  June 1, 2026 completion despite status last verified in October 2024.
  `NCT07190001` still shows an estimated September 30, 2025 start despite status
  last verified in September 2025. These are stale planned dates, not evidence
  that dosing occurred or failed.

## Contradiction And Limits

- The new title and summary include TDT, but the eligibility text supplies no
  TDT diagnostic, transfusion-burden, or genotype definition. Its only
  TDT-specific inclusion text is a washout requirement for selected
  disease-modifying agents. The SCD cohort instead has explicit genotype and
  severity criteria.
- `NCT06678165` is TDT-specific but excludes beta-zero/beta-zero and co-inherited
  alpha-thalassemia. The other two records do not reproduce those exclusions.
  Genotype and phenotype scope cannot be transferred across the records.
- The records share YOLT-204, dose-escalation language, 52-week safety follow-up,
  HbF, intended-modification, and transfusion endpoints. Their differing
  sponsors, ages, protocol identifiers, phase labels, dose descriptions, and
  timelines prevent treating them as one cohort or three independent executed
  studies.
- The new registry identifies intravenous administration and intentionally
  modified alleles, but it does not name the editor, genomic target, complete
  carrier, marrow-targeting evidence, or whether preparative treatment is used.
  Those mechanism details are not inferred.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Mechanism and scope:** The registered intervention is a systemic genetic
  intervention intended to modify alleles detectable in blood and marrow and
  increase HbF. The exact molecular editor, target, delivery formulation, and
  TDT genotype scope remain unresolved.
- **Evidence tier:** Official pre-results clinical-registry evidence. The study
  is not yet recruiting, nonrandomized, open-label, and mixed across TDT and SCD.
- **Hypothesis:** A systemic intervention that produces persistent intended
  modification in marrow and blood could raise HbF and reduce transfusion burden
  without autologous cell collection and individualized ex vivo manufacture.
  The registry does not yet show that this occurs.
- **Interpretation:** Hold YOLT-204 at a three-record systemic
  genetic-intervention registry benchmark only. Do not promote it as clinical
  efficacy, cure, safer conditioning, simpler delivery, active execution, or an
  affordable route.
- **Open question:** Are the three records independent protocols, sequential
  expansions, or superseded versions, and which TDT definition, editor, target,
  carrier, preparative regimen, safety plan, and first-dosed denominator belongs
  to each?
- **Safety risk:** The registered exclusions for oligonucleotide or lipid
  nanoparticle allergy, infection, bleeding, organ dysfunction, splenomegaly,
  iron overload, malignancy, and prior advanced therapy are planned risk
  controls, not observed safety results. The new outcome list does not expose a
  named off-target, clonal-expansion, malignancy, or long-term genomic endpoint.
- **Affordability and access:** No product cost, manufacturing yield, monitoring
  cost, hospital capacity, payer, family cost, Indonesia authorization, or
  access route is reported. A planned intravenous dose does not establish a
  lower delivered cost than ex vivo gene-cell therapy or HSCT.
- **Confidence:** High for the current registry fields and three-record mismatch.
  Low for protocol relations, mechanism details not stated in the registry,
  actual execution, safety, efficacy, durability, affordability, or access.

## Falsification Criterion

Revise the three-record label if official history or protocol documentation
identifies one record as a duplicate, replacement, or extension of another. Do
not promote YOLT-204 beyond registry benchmark until a prospective TDT-specific
cohort reports the screened, enrolled, dosed, and evaluable denominators;
protocol identity; editor, target, carrier, and dose; intended and off-target
editing; clonal and malignancy surveillance; adverse-event attribution;
transfusion and hemoglobin outcomes; durability; and delivered cost. Deprioritize
the lane if the records terminate or remain stale without a documented TDT dose
and no successor protocol resolves the identity gap.

## Next Decisive Action

The YOLT-204 evidence owner should obtain public protocols or responsible-party
clarification for `YOLT-204-IIT`, `YOLT-204-IIT-002`, and
`YOLT-204-IIT-005`. Build one protocol-identity comparison limited to sponsor,
site, age, TDT definition, genotype, editor, target, carrier, dose, preparative
regimen, first-dosed denominator, safety surveillance, transfusion endpoints,
long-term follow-up, and delivered-cost fields. Do not merge planned enrollment
or infer efficacy while those fields remain unresolved. No patient action,
trial-screening, or treatment recommendation follows.
