# Daily Research Journal: 2026-08-04

Audience: founder / researcher
Status: RSUP Dr. Kariadi moves from capacity-only to an adult malignancy HSCT
execution-and-cost-audit benchmark; the thalassemia route remains benchmark only

## Question And Decision

**Question:** Do two 2026 primary studies resolve the morning capacity-only
classification strongly enough to promote RSUP Dr. Kariadi as an Indonesia HSCT
execution and cost benchmark?

**Decision:**
`promote_kariadi_to_adult_malignancy_hsct_execution_and_cost_audit_benchmark_only`.

This replaces the morning capacity-only classification. It establishes
retrospective adult hematologic-malignancy execution and a phase-mapped cost
benchmark. It does not establish a completed current license, a thalassemia
transplant program, durable transfusion independence, acceptable safety, routine
access, affordability, payer coverage, or suitability. Matched-sibling HSCT
remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

**Confidence:** High that Kariadi performed the reported adult HSCT procedures.
Low that the cost values are decision-ready because the paper contains two
incompatible calculation sets. Low that any reviewed source establishes a
thalassemia-specific or currently authorized route.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - execution denominator:** Primary cross-sectional study PMID
  `42052597` reports 45 adults with malignant diseases who underwent HSCT at
  Kariadi from 2013 through 2023. The cohort contained 38 autologous and 7
  allogeneic procedures: 27 multiple myeloma, 7 acute myeloid leukemia, 6
  Hodgkin lymphoma, 3 non-Hodgkin lymphoma, 1 acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and
  1 mixed-phenotype acute leukemia case.
- **Fact - execution endpoint:** PMID `42052597` reports mean neutrophil
  engraftment at 14.29 days and mean platelet engraftment at 13.53 days. This is
  a selected complete-record cohort, not a complete attempted denominator or a
  survival, relapse, GVHD, infection, or long-term safety dataset.
- **Fact - cost design and population:** Primary mixed-method study PMID
  `42345166` uses retrospective cost records for three acute-leukemia
  donor-recipient pairs who underwent allogeneic HSCT at Kariadi in 2023-2024,
  plus interviews with clinical and administrative staff. Donors were related
  or haploidentical family members. The endpoint was activity-based unit cost,
  not clinical efficacy or safety.
- **Fact - mechanism and phenotype scope:** These studies cover autologous and
  allogeneic HSCT for adult malignancies. For acute leukemia, the paper describes
  conditioning plus graft-versus-leukemia activity. Neither study includes a
  thalassemia phenotype, `HBB` genotype, TDT or NTDT classification, donor-
  erythropoiesis endpoint, or transfusion-independence result.
- **Fact - current regulatory signal:** The Ministry of Health's 2026-06-18
  field-verification report and current SIRS profile still establish only a
  licensing-process and facility-listing signal. No formal decision identifier,
  effective date, or thalassemia-specific authorization was found in the exact
  official-source recheck on 2026-08-04.

## Contradictions And Evidence Limits

- **Resolved contradiction - execution versus licensing:** The primary cohorts
  resolve whether Kariadi had performed HSCT before the June 2026 verification.
  It had, for adult malignant diseases. They do not resolve whether the later
  assessment was a new license, renewal, scope expansion, or another
  administrative step, so execution history is not treated as current
  authorization.
- **Unresolved source conflict - total cost:** The cost paper's abstract,
  results narrative, and conclusion report Rp377,978,399 per allogeneic case,
  split into Rp297,063,048 for the recipient and Rp80,915,351 for the donor.
  Table 1 instead totals Rp439,598,636, split into Rp389,880,946 and
  Rp49,717,690. Both sets add correctly but cannot both be the same unit cost.
- **Unresolved source conflict - components and recovery:** The abstract reports
  recipient post-transplant care at Rp157,843,439, donor harvesting at
  Rp37,819,185, recipient tariff cost-recovery ratios of 218.8%-239.0%, and donor
  ratios of 113.7%-123.6%. Tables 1 and 3 instead report Rp235,896,696,
  Rp23,079,311, 167%-182%, and 185%-201%. The narrative's 80.8% and 32.8%
  real-cost recovery also conflicts with Table 2's rounded 60% and 50%.
- **Study limits:** The cost analysis has three pairs from one center, no
  sensitivity analysis, no thalassemia cases, no clinical outcome comparison,
  and no lifetime follow-up cost. The nutrition study combines autologous and
  allogeneic procedures and reports 24 oral plus 16 parenteral nutrition cases
  despite a 45-person cohort. Neither paper supplies a complete center safety
  denominator.
- **Funding and conflict:** The cost paper reports Dr. Kariadi Hospital Grand
  Research funding and no conflict of interest. The nutrition paper reports no
  funding and no conflict of interest. These declarations do not resolve the
  numeric conflicts.

## Cure-Program Implications

- **Hypothesis:** Kariadi could supply a government thalassemia HSCT execution
  route only if current authorization, a thalassemia protocol, complete
  screened-through-followed denominators, long-term outcomes, and reproducible
  delivered cost are independently documented.
- **Interpretation:** Historical adult malignancy execution is now established
  beyond a capacity signal. A phase-mapped Indonesia cost benchmark exists, but
  its internal arithmetic conflict blocks use as an affordability estimate,
  hospital quote, payer amount, or thalassemia cost.
- **Evidence tier and safety risk:** This is single-center retrospective clinical
  and costing evidence. It raises confidence in technical execution, not in
  safety. Missing mortality, graft-failure, infection, GVHD, endocrine,
  fertility, organ, and long-term denominators keep risk unresolved.
- **Open question:** Which cost set is correct, what does each phase include,
  what currency year was used, and what formal authorization followed the June
  2026 assessment?
- **Affordability and access:** The paper states that HSCT was excluded from the
  national insurance benefit package in its study context. This is an author
  statement, not a current BPJS decision. The conflicting unit costs and tariffs
  cannot support an affordable-route claim.
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove the execution benchmark if the center-level
  procedure and transplant-type denominators cannot be reproduced. Keep the cost
  lane audit-only until one corrected calculation set reconciles phase totals,
  cost-recovery formulas, inclusions, currency year, and payer scope. Do not
  promote matched-sibling HSCT beyond `benchmark_only` without thalassemia-
  specific durable transfusion independence, acceptable long-term safety, and
  reproducible delivered cost.
- **Next decisive action:** The health-economics owner should ask the authors and
  journal for a correction plus the de-identified activity-based costing
  workbook that reconciles the abstract, narrative, and tables. No patient
  routing follows.
