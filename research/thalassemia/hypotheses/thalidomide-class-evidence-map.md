# Hypothesis Card: Thalidomide-Class Evidence Map

Status: active high-caution evidence map
Last evidence update: 2026-07-30
Evidence label: not a treatment recommendation

## Claim

Thalidomide-class drugs may reduce transfusion burden in some beta-thalassemia
patients, but their safety profile makes them high-caution candidates.

Current decision:
`hold_thalidomide_at_high_caution_response_benchmark_only`.

## Mechanism

- HbF induction.
- Erythropoiesis and hematopoietic gene-expression effects.
- Possible modulation of pathways including `BCL11A`, `HBS1L-MYB`, `KLF`, and
  erythroid transcription programs.
- Response may vary by genotype and HbF modifier loci.

## Extracted Evidence

- A 2021 multicenter randomized trial reported higher hemoglobin and lower
  transfusion volume versus placebo.
- A smaller adult randomized trial did not support single-agent hydroxyurea or
  low-dose thalidomide as effective options to reduce adult TDT transfusion
  burden.
- A 2025 non-randomized trial reported longer-term hemoglobin improvement, but
  its design is weaker than randomized evidence.
- A second 2026 meta-analysis, PMID `41933128`, pooled heterogeneous single-arm
  response proportions across TDT and NTDT. Only two included studies were
  randomized, and its safety denominators conflict.
- A three-month, uncontrolled Karachi TDT cohort, PMID `42415097`, associated
  `HBG2` `rs7482144`, `BCL11A` `rs766432`, and `HBS1L-MYB` `rs9399137` with
  excellent response. These markers require independent, prespecified
  validation before they can define a responder subgroup.
- A single pediatric NTDT case with autoimmune hemolytic anemia, PMID
  `42526830`, cannot expand the TDT evidence or establish durable response.
- ClinicalTrials.gov shows multiple recent Pakistan-based thalidomide or
  hydroxyurea-plus-thalidomide studies.
- Avadomide has HUDEP2 and selected primary `CD34+` HbF evidence from Yang
  2024, but no first-pass direct thalassemia evidence; keep it as a mechanism
  comparator, not a therapy lead.

## Safety Questions

- Severe teratogenicity.
- Peripheral neuropathy that may be irreversible.
- Venous thromboembolism.
- Sedation and functional impairment.
- Pregnancy prevention requirements.
- Suitability in patients with immune disease.
- Blood count, thrombosis, and neurologic monitoring.

## Falsification Criteria

Reject as an affordable cure lane if benefits require risk controls that are not
realistic in the target access setting, or if adverse-event burden outweighs
transfusion-burden reduction.

Deprioritize even the response-benchmark label if an independent controlled
study fails to reproduce hemoglobin, HbF, or transfusion benefit, or if complete
safety and delivered-cost evidence makes the route infeasible.

## Next Decisive Experiment

A clinical-research owner and independent biostatistics/genomics owner should
preregister one controlled multicenter TDT study with standardized response
definitions, complete participant and safety denominators, 12- to 24-month and
post-withdrawal follow-up, prespecified validation of `HBG2`, `BCL11A`, and
`HBS1L-MYB`, and itemized drug plus monitoring cost.
