# Daily Research Journal: 2026-07-31

Audience: founder / researcher
Status: TTF promoted to a matched-family donor conditioning benchmark only

## Question And Decision

**Question:** Does a new 74-child treosulfan-thiotepa-fludarabine cohort support
promoting TTF as a reduced-toxicity affordable conditioning route across donor
types in TDT?

**Decision:**
`promote_ttf_to_matched_family_donor_conditioning_benchmark_only_pending_adjusted_comparison_safety_and_cost`.

The cohort is material because it supplies contemporary five-year survival
estimates and safety events from India. It does not support donor-agnostic
promotion. The reported five-year overall and thalassemia-free survival
estimates were both
57.3% in the combined matched-unrelated and haploidentical group, compared with
95% and 88.3% in the combined matched-sibling and matched-related group.
Matched-sibling HSCT remains the leading affordable-curative route benchmark
only.

**Confidence:** Moderate that outcomes differ materially by the combined donor
grouping under this TTF program. Low that TTF is less toxic than busulfan,
affordable, or suitable for an alternative-donor route because the study is
retrospective, has no concurrent conditioning comparator, and was reviewed at
abstract level.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact and cohort:** PMID `42467967` is a single-center retrospective analysis
  of 74 children with TDT transplanted in India from January 2017 through May
  2025. Median age at transplant was 6.2 years, with a range of 1 to 18 years.
- **Fact and donor scope:** The cohort included 45 matched-sibling, 4 other
  matched-related, 16 matched-unrelated, and 9 haploidentical donors. All grafts
  used peripheral blood stem cells. The abstract groups the 49 matched-family
  recipients together and the 25 alternative-donor recipients together.
- **Fact and overall outcome:** At median follow-up of 45.2 months, with a range
  of 1.5 to 102 months, five-year overall survival was 82.2% and five-year
  thalassemia-free survival was 77.7%.
- **Fact and donor-stratified outcome:** Five-year overall survival was 95% in
  the combined matched-family group and 57.3% in the combined
  alternative-donor group (`P=0.001`). Five-year thalassemia-free survival was
  88.3% and 57.3%, respectively (`P=0.024`).
- **Fact and graft outcome:** Four children had primary graft failure. Ten had
  mixed chimerism, including two who developed secondary graft failure with
  autologous recovery.
- **Fact and safety:** Acute graft-versus-host disease occurred in 25 children,
  chronic graft-versus-host disease in 7, cytomegalovirus reactivation in 42,
  and veno-occlusive disease in 6.
- **Fact and risk class:** Five-year thalassemia-free survival was 82% for the
  combined Pesaro class I and II group and 65.5% for class III (`P=0.049`).
  Overall survival did not differ significantly by that grouping.

## Contradictions And Evidence Limits

- **Comparator gap:** The authors describe TTF as reduced toxicity and a
  promising alternative to busulfan-based conditioning, but the abstract does
  not report a concurrent busulfan comparator. The study cannot establish that
  TTF caused less toxicity.
- **Donor-group confounding:** The matched group combines matched-sibling and
  other matched-related donors. The alternative group combines
  matched-unrelated and haploidentical donors. The accessible record does not
  expose donor-specific adjusted estimates, so the between-group difference
  cannot be attributed to donor type or conditioning alone.
- **Five-year denominator gap:** Follow-up ranged from 1.5 to 102 months, but
  the abstract does not report the number at risk at five years.
- **Safety-detail gap:** The abstract does not report death count or cause,
  acute or chronic GVHD grade, complete infection burden, ICU use,
  hospitalization, fertility, endocrine outcomes, secondary malignancy, or
  delivered cost.
- **Source-access boundary:** Crossref links only the publisher record, while
  OpenAlex and Unpaywall classify the article as closed with no repository full
  text. Regimen details, supportive care, analysis methods, and complete event
  tables were not reconstructed from references or unrelated protocols.

## Cure-Program Implications

- **Interpretation:** TTF is strong enough to enter the program as a
  matched-family donor conditioning benchmark. The same evidence argues against
  treating it as one donor-agnostic route or as proof of reduced toxicity.
- **Ranking:** Matched-sibling HSCT remains the leading affordable-curative
  route benchmark only. F-BMT remains an alternative-donor conditioning
  benchmark only. This study does not establish superiority between the two
  conditioning programs because donor composition, graft source, population,
  center, supportive care, endpoints, and follow-up differ.
- **Affordability and access:** The paper reports no itemized drug, transplant,
  supportive-care, complication, or follow-up cost. Exact July 31 public BPOM
  product-name and ingredient searches for `treosulfan`, `Trecondi`, `thiotepa`,
  `Tepadina`, `fludarabine`, and `fludarabin` returned no record. This is a
  bounded public-registry gap, not proof of authorization status, hospital
  procurement, unavailability, affordability, or suitability in Indonesia.
- **Hypothesis:** In an independently enrolled matched-family donor pediatric
  cohort, TTF can preserve five-year thalassemia-free survival within a
  preregistered non-inferiority margin while reducing a prespecified composite
  of severe organ toxicity relative to a contemporary busulfan-based protocol.
- **Open question:** Can a donor-stratified comparison reproduce the
  matched-family outcome, explain the alternative-donor result, and show lower
  severe toxicity at an itemized Indonesia-relevant delivered cost?
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove the conditioning-benchmark promotion if
  independent donor-stratified evidence fails the preregistered five-year
  thalassemia-free-survival margin, does not reduce the prespecified severe
  toxicity endpoint, confirms poor alternative-donor survival, or exceeds the
  program's delivered-cost threshold.
- **Next decisive action:** A transplant-program and biostatistics owner should
  obtain the full report and define a prospective donor-stratified comparison
  against one contemporary busulfan-based protocol. It should report five-year
  overall and thalassemia-free survival, number at risk, death and graft-failure
  causes, Grade III or IV GVHD, CMV and invasive infection, veno-occlusive
  disease, ICU and hospitalization, endocrine and fertility outcomes, secondary
  malignancy, and itemized drug, donor-workup, transplant, supportive-care,
  complication, and follow-up cost.
