# Research Status: 2026-08-09

Audience: general research planning
Status: preclinical in vivo HSPC delivery benchmark, not treatment advice

## Question

Does CD34-targeted in vivo HSPC editing close the ex vivo manufacturing and
conditioning gap enough to become an affordable curative route for
beta-thalassemia?

## Decision

`hold_cd34_lnpdp_at_preclinical_in_vivo_hspc_delivery_benchmark_only`

The result is a credible delivery-platform benchmark. It does not establish a
beta-thalassemia phenotype rescue, clinical route, broad safety, scalable
manufacturing, affordability, Indonesia access, or durable cure. A 2025
antibody-free intravenous LNP study remains the closer TDT patient-cell
comparator. A separate short-window beta-thalassemic mouse experiment narrows
the model-provenance gap but did not test the targeted editor or disease rescue.
Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - source and design:** The peer-reviewed Nature Biomedical Engineering
  study, DOI `10.1038/s41551-026-01765-w` and PMID `42557300`, screened 15 LNPs
  and conjugated its selected formulation to an anti-CD34 antibody. The
  therapeutic experiment delivered SpCas9 messenger RNA and a BCL11A-enhancer
  guide by bilateral intrafemoral injection in humanized NCG-X mice.
- **Fact - model and duration:** Mobilized-peripheral-blood human CD34-positive
  HSPCs were engrafted before dosing. Bone marrow was collected 12-16 weeks
  after the intrafemoral injection. The official reporting summary says no
  sample-size calculation was performed, at least three biological replicates
  were used, samples were randomized, investigators were blinded, no data were
  excluded, and only female mice were used.
- **Fact - safety-model split:** Official Extended Data Fig. 9 reports serum
  liver, kidney, and cytokine measurements through 72 hours after bilateral
  intrafemoral `LNPDP-Luc` dosing in four female `HbbTh3/+` beta-thalassemic
  mice. Separate supplementary experiments measured bone-marrow cytokines
  through 48 hours in humanized NOG mice and histology through one week in
  C57BL/6J mice. These experiments used luciferase payloads rather than the
  therapeutic editor, and the beta-thalassemic arm did not use CD34 targeting.
- **Fact - edit and expression endpoints:** The experiment detected persistent
  BCL11A-enhancer indels in human HSPCs and downstream lineages and increased
  `HBG/(HBB+HBG)` messenger RNA in erythroid cells. The measured human-cell
  fraction, lineage distribution, and erythroid-cell fraction did not differ
  significantly across the reported groups.
- **Fact - source transparency:** The publisher provides an official
  supplementary-information PDF, reporting summary, figure source workbooks,
  GEO accession `GSE318716`, and controlled GSA accessions `HRA015967` and
  `HRA016559`. No sequencing analysis was started in this run.
- **Fact - comparator:** The 2025 primary study DOI
  `10.1038/s41551-025-01480-y`, PMID `40796944`, reports antibody-free
  intravenous LNP base editing of HBG1/HBG2 in TDT patient-derived HSPCs in
  humanized mice with a globin-chain-balance endpoint. That study is the closer
  disease-specific and systemic-route comparator.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The 2026 abstract describes suitability for clinical translation, but the
  therapeutic experiment used bilateral intrafemoral injection rather than a
  systemic route. The procedure does not yet remove specialized delivery
  infrastructure.
- The reporting summary identifies mobilized-peripheral-blood human HSPCs but
  does not provide an independent donor count, beta-thalassemia diagnosis,
  genotype, phenotype, or diagnosis-stratified result.
- The study reports no beta-thalassemia anemia, ineffective-erythropoiesis,
  globin-chain-balance, hemolysis, red-cell-lifespan, or transfusion endpoint.
- Supplementary Fig. 17 measures indels at an intended conserved BCL11A target
  across organs. It is not an unbiased genome-wide off-target or clonal-integrity
  analysis.
- The earlier 48-hour cytokine statement combined distinct safety experiments.
  Serum monitoring in `HbbTh3/+` mice ended at 72 hours, bone-marrow cytokine
  sampling in humanized NOG mice ended at 48 hours, and marrow histology in
  C57BL/6J mice ended at one week. The short, split-model, luciferase-payload
  package does not resolve editor-specific or long-term inflammatory, organ,
  genomic, clonal, marrow, fertility, or malignancy risk.
- Anti-CD34 conjugation, LNP formulation, editor messenger RNA, guide RNA, and
  bilateral marrow injection remain material manufacturing and procedural
  burdens. No head-to-head activity-based cost comparison is reported.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** A non-viral in vivo HSPC-editing route could remove apheresis,
  ex vivo manufacture, and myeloablative conditioning if systemic delivery
  reaches long-term repopulating HSCs at a therapeutic fraction without
  unacceptable genomic, clonal, organ, inflammatory, or reproductive harm.
- **Interpretation:** The paper advances delivery feasibility, not
  beta-thalassemia cure evidence. It does not displace the 2025 TDT
  patient-derived antibody-free LNP comparator or any clinical curative route.
- **Open question:** In a powered, genotype-diverse TDT patient-cell model, can
  systemic CD34-targeted delivery outperform antibody-free LNP delivery on
  durable long-term-HSC editing, HbF protein, globin-chain balance, ineffective
  erythropoiesis, hemolysis, red-cell survival, safety, and cost?
- **Affordability and access:** Neither study reports a GMP release path,
  batch-failure denominator, dose-scaled manufacturing cost, hospital procedure
  cost, Indonesia authorization, qualified-center capacity, payer coverage, or
  delivered cost. The affordability ranking is unchanged.

## Falsification Criterion

Deprioritize the CD34-targeted platform if systemic dosing cannot reproduce
durable disease-relevant benefit across at least three independent TDT donors,
if sequence-level or tissue-level editing, clonal behavior, inflammation, organ
findings, or hematopoietic function breach a prespecified safety gate, or if
delivered cost and infrastructure are not lower than the ex vivo comparator.

## Next Decisive Action

An independent preclinical owner should compare CD34-targeted and antibody-free
LNPs head-to-head using the same editor payload, systemic route, dose
accounting, genotype-diverse TDT donor panel, long-term follow-up, broad genomic
safety package with editor-matched controls, disease-relevant endpoints, and an
activity-based delivered-cost model. No patient action, treatment choice, or
sample routing is authorized.
