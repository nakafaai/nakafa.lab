# Research Status: 2026-08-05

Audience: general research planning
Status: source-backed preclinical material-source decision, not treatment advice

## Question

Does the new apheresis-waste preprint close the patient-derived cell-source gap
enough to promote this route into Nakafa Lab's affordable HbF and
globin-chain-balance assay plan?

## Decision

`promote_apheresis_waste_cd34_hspcs_to_preclinical_material_source_benchmark_only`

The new source supports a paired material-source reproduction. It does not
promote a therapy, a cure, a clinical manufacturing route, or an affordable
patient route. All therapeutic and curative-route rankings remain unchanged.

Confidence: moderate for practical recovery feasibility; low for
beta-thalassemia-specific reproducibility, affordability, and Indonesia access.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - source:** BioRxiv version 1, DOI
  `10.64898/2026.08.02.742313`, was posted on 2026-08-04 and is not peer
  reviewed. It reports 14 months of recovery, purification, and banking from
  clinical apheresis product waste and mobilized peripheral blood otherwise
  discarded after clinical procedures.
- **Fact - recovery:** Immunomagnetic selection recovered a median 4.71 million
  CD34-positive cells from 1-2 mL of apheresis product waste. The abstract
  reports a comparable 6.0 million cells from 10-40 times more peripheral-blood
  waste and similar purity across sources and diagnoses.
- **Fact - process signal:** The concentrated apheresis waste did not require
  the density-gradient steps used for peripheral-blood waste, approximately
  halving processing time. Recovered cells engrafted NBSGW mice.
- **Fact - microfluidic pilot:** A separate pilot covered two patients and 11
  specimens and recovered 2.17 million CD34-positive cells per specimen at
  more than 90% viability and purity.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The title and abstract frame the route for beta-hemoglobinopathy research,
  but the indexed abstract does not report the beta-thalassemia donor count,
  genotype, phenotype, or paired donor-level results.
- Mouse engraftment supports preserved repopulating capacity in that model. It
  does not establish erythroid differentiation, HbF, F-cells, total hemoglobin,
  globin-chain balance, free alpha-globin, hemolysis, clinical manufacturing,
  or treatment benefit.
- More than 90% viability and purity describes the small microfluidic pilot. It
  is not a validated release threshold for every source or process.
- Smaller input volume and shorter processing are operational signals. The
  source reports no consumable, labor, storage, quality-control, shipping,
  failure, or cost-per-qualified-aliquot result.
- Existing apheresis infrastructure is a prerequisite. No Indonesia-specific
  governance, center capacity, biobank, shipment, or access route is reported.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** A qualified center with existing apheresis infrastructure,
  compatible consent, ethics approval, and research-only chain of custody can
  convert otherwise discarded beta-thalassemia product waste into a lower-cost,
  multi-donor primary-cell model for Nakafa Lab's assay funnel.
- **Interpretation:** The route can now enter a falsifiable material-source
  comparison. It cannot change a candidate ranking until disease provenance,
  erythroid function, safety, and cost are independently reproduced.
- **Open question:** Can the route recover usable HSPCs from at least three
  genetically distinct beta-thalassemia donors while preserving erythroid and
  globin endpoints and lowering actual cost per qualified aliquot versus the
  current source?
- **Affordability and access:** Waste recovery could reduce acquisition and
  processing burden, but no measured saving exists. Ethics, consent,
  de-identification, biosafety, chain of custody, apheresis infrastructure,
  quality control, and storage remain required inputs.

## Falsification Criterion

Keep or remove the benchmark based on a paired, qualified-center reproduction.
Do not promote beyond `preclinical_material_source_benchmark_only` if:

- beta-thalassemia donor provenance cannot be confirmed;
- research-grade yield, purity, viability, and erythroid function are not
  reproducible across at least three genetically distinct donors;
- recovered cells materially underperform the comparator on colony formation,
  erythroid maturation, enucleation, HbF, F-cells, total hemoglobin,
  globin-chain balance, free alpha-globin, viability, or hemolysis; or
- actual cost per qualified aliquot is not lower than the current source.

## Next Decisive Action

A qualified research center should price and execute one paired feasibility
study with compatible consent, ethics approval, de-identification, biosafety
review, research-only chain of custody, raw-data export, and no patient or
sample routing by Nakafa Lab. The comparison should include donor provenance,
input volume, yield, purity, viability, colony formation, erythroid maturation
and enucleation, HbF and F-cells, total hemoglobin, globin-chain balance, free
alpha-globin, hemolysis, failed-run denominator, and actual staff, consumable,
storage, quality-control, and failure cost.
