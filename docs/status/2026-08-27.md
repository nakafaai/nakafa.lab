# Research Status: 2026-08-27

Audience: general research planning
Status: variant-aware BCL11A guide testing promoted to a preclinical development benchmark only

## Question

Does the August 26 CRISPR-HAWK revision show a clinically observed exa-cel
failure subgroup, or does it only justify a variant-aware preclinical robustness
experiment for the BCL11A enhancer guide?

## Decision

`promote_variant_aware_sg1617_susceptibility_testing_to_preclinical_development_benchmark_only`

The new evidence promotes one bounded development experiment. It does not
establish an exa-cel failure subgroup, treatment-response marker, ancestry rule,
or patient-specific screening decision. Exa-cel remains
`hold_exa_cel_at_strong_curative_benchmark_only_after_process_burden_quantification`.
Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only` for its narrow donor and risk
scope.

## What Changed

BioRxiv lists version 3 of CRISPR-HAWK on 2026-08-26. Its abstract retains the
claim that population variants can abolish predicted activity of `sg1617`, the
BCL11A +58 erythroid-enhancer guide used in exa-cel. The linked public analysis
archive makes the target-level result inspectable.

Reanalysis of the archived table confirms one catalogued single gnomAD
variant with predicted cutting frequency determination score zero. It also
finds that the reported four-sample count for a two-variant sequence is an
arithmetic sum of separate variant carrier counts. It is not an observed count
of people carrying the combined haplotype. This narrows the result from a
clinical subgroup claim to a falsifiable preclinical susceptibility test.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - evidence tier:** DOI `10.64898/2025.12.27.696698`, PMID `41497669`,
  and PMCID `PMC12767513` identify a primary computational preprint. The
  bioRxiv API lists version 3 dated 2026-08-26. The public full text in PMC is
  version 1, so detailed claims were checked against that full text and the
  linked archived outputs rather than assumed to be new in version 3.
- **Fact - analysis scale:** The paper describes 79,648 genomes across 1000
  Genomes, HGDP, and gnomAD. Across seven therapeutic or benchmark loci, 82.5%
  of reference-designed guides contained at least one variant predicted to
  modify on-target activity. This is a percentage of guides, not people or
  clinical failures.
- **Fact - exact target:** In the archived gnomAD result table, reference
  `sg1617` at GRCh38 `chr2:60495261-60495284` has predicted residual on-target
  activity `1.0`. Variant `chr2-60495268-T/G` has dataset population code
  `afr`, reported allele frequency `0.00002`, three computed carriers, and
  predicted residual activity `0.0`.
- **Fact - other target variants:** `chr2-60495273-G/A` has reported allele
  frequency `0.000007`, one computed carrier, and predicted residual activity
  `0.3077`. `chr2-60495283-G/C` is labeled non-Finnish European, has reported
  allele frequency `0.000007`, one computed carrier, and predicted residual
  activity `0.913`.
- **Fact - reproducibility:** The inspected `results_nsamples.tar.gz` archive
  is 3,129,551 bytes and matched the Zenodo MD5 checksum
  `b4ee3b17468bb7764fb236cd8a4018f0`. The exact filter was the `sg1617` start
  and stop pair in the gnomAD, HGDP, and 1000 Genomes BCL11A result tables.
  HGDP and 1000 Genomes contained only the reference row at that exact target.
- **Fact - prediction method:** CRISPR-HAWK repurposes the CFD off-target model
  to predict residual on-target activity when a variant creates a mismatch.
  The study did not edit a TDT donor cell, measure BCL11A, HbF, globin-chain
  balance, transfusion independence, or a clinical outcome.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The paper calls the combined `chr2-60495268-T/G` plus
  `chr2-60495273-G/A` sequence a haplotype with four samples and predicted CFD
  zero. The public sample-count script calculates that value by adding the
  separate carrier counts, three plus one. Aggregated gnomAD site data do not
  show that four people carried both variants together.
- The single `chr2-60495268-T/G` result remains a catalogued rare variant in the
  archived gnomAD table, but its zero activity is a model prediction. No
  biochemical or cellular experiment validates zero cutting.
- The analysis does not include a TDT cohort, an Indonesia-specific cohort, or
  treatment recipients. Database population labels cannot become ancestry,
  eligibility, access, or outcome rules.
- The current bioRxiv record is version 3, while PMC, Zenodo, and the manuscript
  reproduction scripts expose the earlier full analysis package. The version 3
  abstract repeats the core values, but the retrieved evidence does not prove
  that the underlying data or result changed on August 26.
- The public workflow is reproducible from archived outputs, but a complete
  from-scratch rerun requires about 3.66 GB of reference and variant inputs plus
  the CRISPR-HAWK environment. No tool was installed and no large archive was
  downloaded for this bounded verification.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Fact:** One rare catalogued target variant is predicted to abolish `sg1617`
  activity, and another is predicted to reduce it. This is a preclinical risk
  signal, not clinical response evidence.
- **Fact - late process comparator:** BioRxiv DOI
  `10.64898/2026.08.26.747413` is a version 1 primary preclinical preprint dated
  2026-08-27. Its official abstract directly compares electroporation delivery
  of Cas9 RNP with LNP delivery of Cas9 messenger RNA in primary HSPCs from
  healthy HbAA donors and people with SCD. Electroporation produced higher
  editing, while the LNP groups retained greater viability and cell yield
  through in vitro erythroid differentiation. The abstract reports HbF in
  proportion to indel frequency and reduced in vitro sickling after LNP
  editing, not a TDT, clinical, or curative outcome.
- **Hypothesis:** A low-cost variant-aware preflight test can identify target
  sequences that materially reduce `sg1617` editing before expensive
  individualized HSPC manufacture, while a validated alternative guide can
  recover editing without unacceptable genomic risk or process cost.
- **Interpretation:** Promote variant-aware `sg1617` susceptibility testing to a
  preclinical development benchmark only. The late LNP preprint sharpens the
  delivery comparator but does not validate `sg1617`, identify the guide used
  in its accessible abstract, or justify a separate LNP promotion. Keep exa-cel
  and the broader gene-cell route rankings unchanged.
- **Open question:** Does the catalogued `chr2-60495268-T/G` target actually
  reduce same-process `sg1617` editing, BCL11A enhancer disruption, and HbF
  induction in an erythroid or HSPC model, and can a rescue guide restore those
  endpoints safely and at lower total process cost than an uninformative or
  failed manufacturing attempt?
- **Affordability and access:** A preflight assay could avoid wasted collection
  and manufacturing only if the prediction changes a validated process
  decision. Rare prevalence, confirmatory testing, alternative-guide
  qualification, release testing, and regulator review may erase any saving.
  No source reports cost, payer coverage, BPOM acceptance, Indonesia laboratory
  capacity, or reduced failed-lot burden.
- **Confidence:** High that the public archived output and sample-count code
  support the narrow computational result and its carrier-count caveat.
  Moderate that the catalogued single variant merits a preclinical robustness
  experiment. Low for clinical failure, population prevalence in TDT, ancestry
  effects, affordability, or Indonesia transferability.
- **Late-evidence limits:** The accessible LNP abstract omits independent donor
  counts, the exact guide and target coordinates, LNP composition and dose,
  statistical detail, long-term repopulation, off-target and structural-variant
  testing, failed-batch counts, and cost. The publisher PDF returned HTTP 429
  during this check, so no full-text detail is inferred.

## Falsification Criterion

Reject the predicted failure signal if blinded, same-process testing shows that
the catalogued `chr2-60495268-T/G` target does not cause a prespecified and
reproducible loss of on-target editing relative to the reference sequence.
Reject the affordable-process hypothesis if target screening plus rescue-guide
qualification costs more than the failures it prevents, does not change a
manufacturing decision, or introduces unacceptable off-target, structural,
viability, erythroid, or long-term repopulation risk. Do not promote LNP
delivery if its viability or cell-yield advantage disappears after normalizing
for viable edited long-term repopulating HSPCs, or if it adds unacceptable
genomic risk or total process cost.

## Next Decisive Action

A qualified gene-editing and HSPC laboratory owner should run a staged test
with the same research-grade `sg1617` RNP across reference,
`chr2-60495268-T/G`, `chr2-60495273-G/A`, and explicitly synthetic combined
target contexts. Start with an isogenic target assay. Only if it confirms the
target effect, compare electroporation of `sg1617` RNP with LNP delivery of
Cas9 messenger RNA plus the same guide in an erythroid model and at least three
independent genotype-diverse TDT HSPC backgrounds. Use a non-targeting control
and a variant-matched rescue guide. Measure on-target editing, BCL11A
expression, HbF protein, globin-chain balance, erythroid maturation, viability,
colony output, long-term repopulation, structural variation, off-target
activity, failed runs, hands-on time, consumables, and cost per decision-ready
batch. No patient action, eligibility conclusion, treatment recommendation,
referral, purchase, or sample routing follows.
