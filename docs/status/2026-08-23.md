# Research Status: 2026-08-23

Audience: general research planning
Status: persistent AAV-Cas9 deprioritized as an affordable in vivo TDT-editing architecture

## Question

Does newly indexed 10-month AAV8-SaCas9 liver evidence support persistent
nuclease expression as a viable affordable in vivo TDT-editing architecture?

## Decision

`deprioritize_persistent_aav_cas9_as_affordable_in_vivo_tdt_editing_architecture_after_long_term_mouse_liver_safety_signal`

Persistent AAV-Cas9 expression is retained as a long-term mouse-liver safety
hazard benchmark only. CD34-targeted messenger-RNA LNP editing remains
`preclinical_in_vivo_hspc_delivery_benchmark_only`. YOLT-204 remains a
pre-results systemic genetic-intervention benchmark because its editor and
carrier are undisclosed. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only` for its narrow donor and risk
scope.

## What Changed

PubMed newly indexed PMID `42629331`, a publisher-accepted Protein & Cell
manuscript with a linked supplement. The study follows persistent AAV8-SaCas9
editing in mouse liver for 10 months and adds immune, fibrotic, mutational,
integration, and translocation signals that predicted off-target-site testing
alone would miss. This changes the affordable in vivo editing experiment:
editor exposure duration must be matched with efficacy, broad genomic safety,
clonal surveillance, and total cost.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - model and exposure:** Eight-week-old male C57BL/6J mice received one
  retro-orbital dose containing 1 to 2 times 10^13 AAV8 viral genomes. The
  vector expressed Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 and a `Pcsk9`, `Angptl3`, or
  non-targeting guide. This is persistent viral liver editing, not transient
  messenger-RNA delivery.
- **Fact - intended editing:** `Pcsk9-G1` reached 42.1 plus or minus 5.9% liver
  indels at month 10. The main target groups used 10 mice each and the
  non-targeting group used eight.
- **Fact - immune and tissue signal:** Ten of 39 Cas9-exposed mice exceeded the
  study's high anti-SaCas9 antibody threshold. High antibody levels were
  associated with ALT activity, greater T-cell and macrophage infiltration,
  larger fibrotic area, and lower normalized editing. Overall liver morphology
  and body weight remained similar, and no malignant transformation was
  observed by the endpoint.
- **Fact - mutation signal:** Whole-exome sequencing covered 21 liver libraries
  at a reported mean depth near 155 times. The authors report 181 genes with
  non-silent mutations across edited groups and frequent `Atm` and `Atr`
  mutations in the `Angptl3-G4` group.
- **Fact - structural signal:** Deep sequencing of the top ten predicted
  off-target sites found no significant indel increase. Target-enrichment
  sequencing nevertheless detected on-target AAV integration and translocations
  above baseline between intended cuts and low-homology genomic regions in the
  highest-edited animals.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The authors interpret the low-frequency variants as early clonal selection,
  but no lineage tracing directly establishes expansion of individual clones.
- One PBS-treated mouse served as the shared germline reference for all 21
  whole-exome libraries. Other PBS and non-targeting controls retained
  substantial called mutation burdens, so the study does not provide a simple
  treatment-versus-control mutation-rate estimate.
- The main differential-expression screen used raw `P < 0.05` because an
  adjusted threshold yielded too few genes for downstream analysis. This makes
  the transcriptomic selection argument exploratory.
- Only male mice and liver were studied. The evidence does not estimate risk in
  human HSPCs, beta-thalassemia cells, erythroid lineages, a TDT disease model,
  or a transient messenger-RNA LNP architecture.
- No malignant transformation was observed. The result is a safety signal and
  experimental-design decision, not a cancer-causation claim.
- The publisher marks the article as an accepted manuscript that can still
  change during copyediting and typesetting, although its DOI is stable.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Mechanism and scope:** Persistent bacterial-nuclease expression can maintain
  intended editing while adding immune and DNA-break exposure. The new evidence
  concerns exposure architecture, not TDT efficacy or durable transfusion
  independence.
- **Evidence tier:** Publisher-accepted primary mouse study, official supplement,
  and PubMed metadata.
- **Hypothesis:** Transient editor exposure can preserve durable intended HSPC
  modification while reducing anti-editor immunity, clonal selection, AAV
  integration, and structural-variation burden relative to persistent AAV
  expression.
- **Interpretation:** Deprioritize persistent AAV-Cas9 expression as an
  affordable in vivo TDT-editing architecture. Do not transfer this signal to
  messenger-RNA LNPs or YOLT-204 without matching editor, carrier, exposure,
  tissue, dose, and follow-up.
- **Open question:** In a TDT-relevant long-term HSPC model, can transient
  systemic editor delivery preserve disease-relevant benefit while reducing
  immune, clonal, insertional, and structural-variation burden relative to the
  same editor and guide under persistent expression?
- **Safety risk:** Predicted off-target-site testing alone is insufficient.
  Future work must include editor and carrier immunity, clonal composition,
  DNA-damage-response mutations, integration, translocations, organ and
  fertility injury, and malignancy surveillance.
- **Affordability and access:** A single injection is not an affordability
  result. Persistent-editor manufacture, immune management, long-term genomic
  surveillance, and failed-dose or retreatment constraints can add cost. No
  source reports a TDT delivered cost, Indonesia authorization, local
  manufacture, payer coverage, or qualified-center capacity.
- **Confidence:** High that the study adds a long-term safety signal for its
  persistent AAV8-SaCas9 male mouse-liver model. Moderate that persistent editor
  exposure should be deprioritized in affordable TDT route design. Low for any
  quantitative transfer to HSPCs, people, transient delivery, or cancer risk.

## Falsification Criterion

Keep persistent AAV-Cas9 out of the affordable route ranking unless an
exposure-matched TDT-relevant HSPC and large-animal program shows durable
disease rescue without higher editor immunity, inflammation, fibrosis, clonal
dominance, DNA-damage-response mutation, integration, translocation, organ
injury, fertility injury, or malignancy than transient delivery, and demonstrates
a lower activity-based delivered cost. Deprioritize the transient route too if
the same long-term hazards persist despite bounded editor exposure.

## Next Decisive Action

A qualified gene-editing and HSPC-safety owner should compare persistent and
transient delivery using the same editor, guide, intended edit, and dose
accounting. The study should include at least three genotype-diverse TDT donors,
durable repopulating-HSPC and erythroid endpoints, HbF protein and globin-chain
balance, ineffective erythropoiesis and hemolysis, broad genomic and clonal
assays, editor and carrier immunity, organ and fertility safety, failed-run
denominators, and activity-based delivered cost. No patient action, treatment
recommendation, referral, purchase, or sample routing follows.
