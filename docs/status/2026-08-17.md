# Research Status: 2026-08-17

Audience: general research planning
Status: pediatric gene-therapy collection burden corrected and held at benchmark only

## Question

Does the current 13-child TDT collection series show that pediatric ex vivo gene
therapy can avoid repeat stem-cell collection and support an affordable-curative
route promotion?

## Decision

`promote_pediatric_tdt_gene_therapy_collection_to_corrected_process_burden_benchmark_only`

Beti-cel and exa-cel retain their existing curative-benchmark and process-burden
holds. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## What Changed

The same-day PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov queries returned no new curative or
disease-modifying result. A source reconciliation for PMID `42091200` found that
its PubMed English abstract conflicts with the current official publisher
abstract.

The publisher record resolves the 13-child denominator and quantifies a pediatric
collection burden. It reports 25 collection procedures, repeat collection in 8 of
13 children, and one failed adequate-cell collection after two procedures. The
evidence strengthens the route-level collection and capacity hold. It does not
establish a product-specific rate, manufacturing success, durable transfusion
independence, affordability, access, or individual suitability.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - design and scope:** PMID `42091200`, DOI
  `10.3760/cma.j.cn511374-20250616-00372`, is a peer-reviewed, single-center
  Chinese-language report of 13 children with TDT undergoing stem-cell
  collection for gene therapy from August 2020 through January 2024.
- **Fact - corrected collection denominator:** The current official publisher
  abstract reports five children with one collection, six with two, and two with
  four. These strata sum to all 13 children and 25 collection procedures. Eight
  of 13 children underwent more than one collection.
- **Fact - adequate-cell result:** Twelve children obtained adequate cells. One
  child did not obtain adequate CD34-positive cells after two collections.
- **Fact - bounded safety result:** The abstract reports no adverse reaction
  during collection among the 13 children. This covers the collection procedure
  only, not mobilization, manufacturing, conditioning, infusion, or long-term
  gene-therapy safety.

## Contradiction And Limits

- PubMed currently states that one child had one collection, nine had two, one
  had three, and one had four. Those strata enumerate only 12 children and imply
  26 procedures without placing the failed thirteenth child. The official
  publisher abstract instead enumerates all 13 and states that the failed child
  had two collections. The publisher denominator is used; the PubMed abstract is
  retained as conflicting metadata and is not pooled with it.
- The accessible publisher record exposes the bilingual abstract and an
  introductory trial section. The full methods, results, tables, and PDF require
  access, so collection target, per-procedure CD34-positive yield, mobilization
  details, age distribution, genotype, splenic status, baseline transfusion and
  iron burden, and complete adverse-event definitions were not verified.
- The report does not identify the gene-therapy product or editing modality. It
  does not report manufacturing release, failed lot, infusion, conditioning,
  transfusion independence, follow-up, delivered cost, payer payment, Indonesia
  access, or center throughput.
- The authors recommend stronger mobilization and a pre-collection transfusion
  strategy. Without comparative methods or results, that recommendation is not
  adopted as an intervention-effect claim or patient advice.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Mechanism and scope:** Ex vivo gene-cell therapy requires enough autologous
  HSPCs for the therapeutic product and backup cells. This source measures the
  collection step in one pediatric TDT center, not a complete gene-therapy
  pathway.
- **Evidence tier:** Peer-reviewed single-center human implementation evidence,
  limited to the current publisher abstract and introductory text.
- **Hypothesis:** A product-stratified pediatric collection workflow using a
  prespecified adequate-cell threshold and first-collection yield model can
  reduce repeat procedures and cost per released lot without increasing failed
  collections or collection adverse events.
- **Interpretation:** Promote the source to a corrected pediatric process-burden
  benchmark only. Do not promote pediatric ex vivo gene therapy as simpler,
  lower cost, more available, or safer than adult gene-cell therapy or
  matched-sibling HSCT.
- **Open question:** Which product, mobilization protocol, cell target, yield,
  starting denominator, release result, and cost correspond to each of the 25
  procedures?
- **Confidence:** High for the current publisher abstract's arithmetic and the
  PubMed contradiction. Moderate that repeat collection is a material pediatric
  capacity burden. Low for product-specific transfer, safety beyond collection,
  manufacturing success, affordability, access, or clinical outcome.
- **Affordability and access:** Twenty-five procedures for 13 children establish
  repeated resource use, not its price or affordability. No itemized staff,
  consumable, admission, manufacturing, failed-lot, payer, or family cost is
  reported.

## Falsification Criterion

Remove or revise the benchmark if the full report or an author or publisher
correction changes the 13-child, 25-procedure, 12-adequate, or one-failure
denominators. Do not promote an optimized pediatric collection route unless a
consecutive, product-stratified cohort reports all screened children, collection
attempts and yields, released and failed lots, adverse events, time, and itemized
cost and shows fewer repeat procedures without more collection failure or safety
burden than a prespecified same-product comparator.
