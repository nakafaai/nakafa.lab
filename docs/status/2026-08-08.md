# Research Status: 2026-08-08

Audience: general research planning
Status: corrected early clinical editing benchmark, not treatment advice

## Question

Does the formal correction to the reni-cel phase 1-2 report leave it reliable
enough for promotion beyond an early clinical HBG-promoter-editing benchmark?

## Decision

`hold_reni_cel_at_corrected_early_clinical_hbg_promoter_editing_benchmark_only`

The correction resolves material engraftment, safety, and mechanism-label
contradictions in the primary report. The corrected evidence preserves an early
clinical HbF and transfusion-independence benchmark. It does not support an
active development route, durable cure, acceptable long-term safety,
affordability, Indonesia access, or suitability. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - source and design:** The primary NEJM report, DOI
  `10.1056/NEJMoa2501277`, is a sponsor-funded, multicenter, open-label,
  single-group phase 1-2 study. Development was discontinued and the study
  terminated early. The published analysis of all nine treated adults with TDT
  was not prespecified. Eleven participants completed mobilization and
  apheresis; nine were treated. One of the other two withdrew and one became
  ineligible for conditioning after a Crohn disease diagnosis. No manufacturing
  failure was reported for those two exclusions.
- **Fact - mechanism and genotype scope:** Reni-cel uses autologous
  CD34-positive HSPCs edited with AsCas12a at BCL11A binding sites in the
  `HBG1` and `HBG2` promoters to reactivate HbF. The cohort included three
  beta-zero/beta-zero, one beta-plus/beta-plus with homozygous IVS-I-110 G-to-A,
  four beta-zero/beta-plus, and one HbE/beta-zero participant. The observed age
  range was 18 to 29 years.
- **Fact - corrected engraftment:** The formal NEJM correction, DOI
  `10.1056/NEJMx260013` and PMID `42555951`, states that all nine participants
  had neutrophil engraftment by day 42. It removes platelet engraftment from
  that sentence. Table 1 reports platelet engraftment from day 23 through day
  49.
- **Fact - efficacy boundary:** All nine participants were transfusion-free at
  their last observed visit, for a median 17.0 months and a range of 3.1 to
  22.7 months. Only six had enough follow-up to be evaluated for at least 12
  months of transfusion independence.
- **Fact - corrected safety:** The correction replaces incorrect narrative
  rates with decreased platelet count in 5 of 9 participants across 13 events,
  decreased neutrophil count in 5 of 9 across 12 events, and stomatitis in 6 of
  9 across 6 events. The report records 69 grade 3 or 4 events in nine
  participants and six serious events in four participants. No death or
  malignancy was reported by the data cutoff.
- **Fact - target-name correction:** The correction changes one Discussion
  reference from a GATA1 binding site to the BCL11A binding site, aligning it
  with the Methods and product design.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The original abstract joined neutrophil and platelet engraftment at day 42,
  while Table 1 reported platelet engraftment through day 49. The correction
  removes platelet engraftment from the day-42 claim.
- The original Results narrative reported common adverse events as 7%, 6%, and
  3% of nine participants. The correction restores the Table 2 denominators of
  56%, 56%, and 67%, with event counts of 13, 12, and 6.
- The current public `NCT05444894` record remains active, not recruiting, with
  no posted results, while the paper says development was discontinued in
  December 2024 and the study terminated early. The registry status cannot
  establish active development.
- The 15-year `NCT06363760` observational follow-up is enrolling by invitation
  and has no posted results. Planned follow-up is not observed durability or
  safety evidence.
- The small cohort, nonprespecified analysis, median 17.5-month postinfusion
  follow-up, and full busulfan and transplant pathway do not resolve late
  genomic, clonal, marrow, malignancy, organ, endocrine, fertility, or
  manufacturing risk.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** Corrected long-term participant-level follow-up can retain
  reni-cel as a mechanism and durability comparator if editing, HbF,
  hematopoiesis, and transfusion independence remain stable without
  unacceptable late harm.
- **Interpretation:** The correction strengthens source traceability and
  preserves the early efficacy signal, but it makes the safety burden less
  favorable than the original narrative implied. Reni-cel remains an early
  clinical benchmark only.
- **Open question:** Across every treated TDT participant, what are the
  corrected platelet-engraftment denominator, product-related and
  conditioning-related events, five-year transfusion status, editing
  persistence, malignancy and clonal findings, marrow function, organ,
  endocrine, fertility, manufacturing-failure, and delivered-cost outcomes?
- **Affordability and access:** The sources provide no itemized manufacturing
  cost, scaled commercial manufacturing-failure rate, delivered price,
  Indonesia authorization, qualified-center capacity, payer coverage, or total
  follow-up cost. Discontinued development prevents use as a current access
  route.

## Falsification Criterion

Deprioritize even the early clinical benchmark if corrected participant-level
follow-up does not sustain editing, HbF, or transfusion independence, or if
death, malignancy, clonal expansion, marrow failure, organ harm, endocrine or
fertility harm, or manufacturing failure makes the risk-benefit profile
unacceptable. Do not promote it as an affordable route without itemized
delivered cost and a reproducible access pathway.

## Next Decisive Action

The research evidence owner should reconcile the correction against the
abstract, Results narrative, Table 1, Table 2, `NCT05444894`, and
`NCT06363760`, then obtain a complete corrected participant-level long-term
denominator. That package must separate product, busulfan, transplant, and
follow-up harms before any ranking change.
