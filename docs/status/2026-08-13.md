# Research Status: 2026-08-13

Audience: general research planning
Status: exa-cel process-comparison decision, not treatment advice

## Question

Do adult TDT trial and FDA manufacturing records establish a collection or
manufacturing advantage for exa-cel over beti-cel that is strong enough to
change the affordable-curative route ranking?

## Decision

`hold_exa_cel_at_strong_curative_benchmark_only_after_process_burden_quantification`

No comparative process advantage is established. Exa-cel remains a strong
curative benchmark, beti-cel remains a durable long-term
transfusion-independence benchmark, and matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

This update changes the exa-cel process evidence from a generic requirement to
an observed collection and manufacturing burden. It does not change the route
ranking because the available exa-cel and beti-cel sources use different
denominators, exact exa-cel lot-failure counts are redacted, and neither route
has a complete commercial referral-to-infusion, delivered-cost, or Indonesia
implementation dataset.

## What Changed

The August 12 decision required identical process fields for beti-cel,
exa-cel, and matched-sibling HSCT. The current repository already contained the
exa-cel efficacy and pediatric safety conclusions, but it did not integrate the
adult TDT collection denominator or the FDA chemistry, manufacturing, and
controls review of clinical lot failures.

The FDA TDT approval archive supplies two decision-relevant findings. First,
all 59 adult-trial participants started mobilization, but 52 received exa-cel.
Second, FDA's manufacturing reviewer described a relatively high clinical-lot
failure rate and multiple-lot use, while redacting the exact counts and rates.
These findings block any assumption that nonviral editing removes the
collection or individualized-manufacturing bottleneck.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - mobilization-to-infusion denominator:** In adult TDT trial
  `NCT03655678`, 59 participants started mobilization, 53 started busulfan
  conditioning, and 52 received exa-cel at the January 16, 2023 interim
  cutoff. The FDA statistical review documents three discontinuations after
  mobilization and before conditioning: withdrawal of consent for undisclosed
  reasons, a wish to undergo a second apheresis procedure, and concern about
  continued participation.
- **Interpretation - unresolved interim dispositions:** The visible FDA disposition
  snapshot does not assign a final status to the other four mobilized
  participants who were outside the 52-person infused group and the three
  documented discontinuations. The 52 of 59 ratio is therefore an interim
  process snapshot, not a final manufacturing-success or treatment-throughput
  rate.
- **Fact - repeat collection among treated participants:** The peer-reviewed
  adult trial reports that 81% of the 52 infused participants completed one
  mobilization cycle. The remaining 19% required more than one, with a range of
  one to four cycles. The current FDA label reports a mean of 1.3 cycles,
  standard deviation 0.7, and a median of one, range one to four, for product
  manufacture and backup-cell collection.
- **Fact - commercial collection requirements:** The current FDA label
  recommends a total collection target of at least 20 million CD34-positive
  cells per kilogram, permits up to three consecutive collection days per
  cycle, requires additional cycles if the minimum exa-cel dose is not met,
  and requires at least 2 million unmodified backup CD34-positive cells per
  kilogram before myeloablation.
- **Fact - manufacturing failure signal:** The FDA CMC review states that
  multiple exa-cel product lots were needed for some participants. A separate
  information request supplied clinical-lot failure data, but the total lot
  count, rejected-lot count, indication-specific rates, and one failure cause
  are redacted. The reviewer nevertheless describes the failure rate as
  relatively high and identifies visible causes including incoming starting-
  material contamination and inability to obtain enough cells. The reviewer
  also warns that corrected particulate-inspection handling could increase
  near-term lot failures.
- **Fact - current registry boundary:** `NCT03655678` is completed with 59
  actual enrollees and no posted results in the ClinicalTrials.gov record
  checked on 2026-08-13. Registry completion does not resolve the redacted
  manufacturing denominator or provide a commercial cohort.

## Contradictions And Limits

- FDA concluded that the validated exa-cel process and controls can produce
  product with consistent quality. That conclusion applies to process control
  and accepted product. It does not erase the separate attempted-lot failure
  denominator described by the CMC reviewer.
- The CMC batch section states that the released clinical lots in its described
  analysis met the release criteria and were administered. The later failure-
  rate section separately includes rejected attempts. Released lots and all
  attempted lots are different denominators.
- The exact exa-cel clinical-lot failure rate is redacted. The qualitative FDA
  concern is decision-relevant, but it cannot be converted into a percentage or
  compared numerically with beti-cel.
- Exa-cel and beti-cel collection evidence is not denominator-aligned. Exa-cel
  reports cycle distribution among infused participants, while the beti-cel
  parent analysis reports collection-target attainment among all 66 who
  initiated mobilization.
- Neither product has a reviewed commercial cohort beginning with all
  referrals. Trial efficacy denominators must not be used as commercial
  throughput or affordability denominators.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** Exa-cel could improve the affordable-curative ranking only if
  a transferable commercial process shows lower referral-to-infusion
  attrition, fewer repeated collections and failed lots, less conditioning and
  admission burden, and a credible total-cost and capacity advantage over
  beti-cel while approaching matched-sibling HSCT.
- **Interpretation:** Keep exa-cel at a strong curative benchmark. The new
  process synthesis rejects an assumed manufacturing advantage and requires
  attempted-lot, not only released-lot, accounting in every future comparison.
- **Open question:** Across all commercial TDT referrals, what proportions
  reach authorization, mobilization, adequate collection, released product,
  infusion, and durable transfusion independence, and what time, failure,
  hospital, surveillance, and payer cost accumulates at each step?
- **Confidence:** High that repeat collection, multiple-lot use, and clinical-
  lot failure occurred. Moderate that the adult-trial collection burden applies
  to experienced centers. Low for comparative commercial throughput,
  affordability, transferability, and Indonesia access because rates are
  redacted or absent.
- **Affordability and access:** Nonviral editing does not remove mobilization,
  apheresis, backup-cell collection, individualized manufacture, full
  myeloablation, specialist admission, or long-term surveillance. No reviewed
  source supplies an itemized delivered cost or Indonesia route. Process burden
  is not itself a monetary affordability estimate.

## Falsification Criterion

Deprioritize exa-cel as an affordable-route candidate if a complete commercial
referral-to-durable-outcome cohort shows persistent collection or lot failure,
unacceptable conditioning or admission burden, or no credible activity-based
total-cost and capacity advantage over beti-cel while approaching the matched-
sibling HSCT benchmark. Re-rank immediately for a confirmed serious genomic
safety signal, material loss of durable transfusion independence, or a
nonrecoverable commercial manufacturing-failure pattern.

## Next Decisive Action

A research evidence owner should first obtain the full report and any supplement
for PMID `42587379`. Extract the independent pediatric TDT denominator,
genotype and phenotype, donor type, conditioning, transplant and outcome
denominators, graft failure, graft-versus-host disease, deaths, follow-up, late
effects, conflicts, and any cost or access data. Do not use the title or letter
metadata as outcome evidence.

Then obtain one owner-verified commercial dataset for exa-cel and one for
beti-cel using the same unit of analysis and starting with every TDT referral.
Require authorization and denial, mobilization and apheresis cycles, attempted,
released, rejected and discarded lots, recollection, manufacture and release
time, bridge care, conditioning, admission and intensive-care days,
complications, fertility preservation, readmission, surveillance, durable
outcome, and itemized payer-relevant cost. Compare the same fields with the
narrow matched-sibling HSCT benchmark. No patient action, referral, or sample
routing is authorized.

## Current Source Sweep

- **Fact - PubMed:** The final exact thalassemia electronic-date query rose
  from two to five records. PMID `42587379` is indexed as a letter titled as a
  two-decade single-center pediatric TDT HSCT experience. PubMed provides no
  abstract, and the retrieved PubMed and Crossref metadata expose no cohort
  size, donor types, genotype, endpoint, follow-up, safety result, cost, access,
  or conflict statement. It is therefore a source-acquisition priority, not an
  outcome. PMID `42586156` is a diagnostic machine-learning review, and PMID
  `42587656` is an observational biomarker study in beta-thalassemia trait. The
  broader globin query rose from one to three records because PMID `42591029`
  is a diagnostic CBC-index study and PMID `42586456` is a fish-infection
  study. None supplies a denominator-aligned exa-cel, beti-cel, or HSCT result.
- **Fact - registry:** The final ClinicalTrials.gov date query rose from one to
  five records. The only new curative-route record was alternative-donor HSCT
  study `NCT03653338`, an open-label, single-group phase 1/2 study estimating
  five participants across sickle cell disease, beta-thalassemia major, and
  Diamond-Blackfan anemia. Version 15 moved estimated primary completion from
  August 2026 to August 2028 and estimated study completion from August 2027 to
  August 2028. Recruiting status, enrollment, outcomes, and no-results status
  were unchanged. Its primary endpoints cover graft rejection, treatment-
  related mortality, and acute and chronic graft-versus-host disease, but no
  beta-thalassemia-specific participant or result is posted. The other records
  were haemophilia, sickle-cell or sickle-beta mechanism, continued-access, or
  disease-modifying studies without a new TDT curative outcome. The current
  `NCT03655678` record remains completed with 59 actual enrollees and no posted
  results.
- **Fact - regulator:** The FDA-hosted Casgevy package insert was rechecked in
  its July 2026 revision. It retains the adult TDT 59-started and 52-infused
  denominator and the collection, backup-cell, additional-cycle, and full-
  myeloablation requirements used above.
- **Fact - preprints:** Final cursor-complete API passes retrieved all 238
  bioRxiv and 109 medRxiv records then visible in the August 12-13 interval.
  The broad screen returned a protoglobin biocatalysis study, an alpha-globin
  protein-sequence method, an acrylamide hemoglobin-adduct study, and a lymphoma
  pathology model. None studied thalassemia, a curative intervention, or a
  decision endpoint. These live counts are a checked snapshot, not a stable
  publication total.
- **Interpretation:** The late HSCT citation and registry delay do not provide
  an outcome that can overturn the process comparison. Preserve
  `hold_exa_cel_at_strong_curative_benchmark_only_after_process_burden_quantification`,
  the beti-cel durability benchmark, and the narrow matched-sibling HSCT route
  ranking.
- **Open question:** Does the full PMID `42587379` report contain a
  genotype-resolved, donor-resolved pediatric TDT comparator with complete
  transplant, survival, graft, late-safety, and affordability denominators?
- No BCL, FASTQ, BAM, CRAM, VCF, count matrix, or essential sequencing metadata
  was present. No NGS analysis was started or inferred.
