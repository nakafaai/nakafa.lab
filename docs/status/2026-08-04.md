# Daily Research Journal: 2026-08-04

Audience: founder / researcher
Status: RSUP Dr. Kariadi adds a government HSCT capacity-development benchmark;
the curative route remains benchmark only

## Question And Decision

**Question:** Does the Ministry of Health field verification at RSUP Dr. Kariadi
establish a government-channel thalassemia HSCT route strongly enough to promote
matched-sibling HSCT beyond `benchmark_only`?

**Decision:**
`promote_kariadi_to_government_hsct_capacity_development_benchmark_only`.

This is a government-hospital capacity-development signal, not evidence of a
completed license, thalassemia transplant program, durable transfusion
independence, routine access, acceptable safety, affordability, payer coverage,
or suitability. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

The source was published on 2026-06-18 and is newly integrated on 2026-08-04.
It is not a new event today.

**Confidence:** High that the field verification occurred and that the current
government facility profile lists bone marrow transplantation. Low that the
public evidence establishes completed authorization or thalassemia-specific
delivery.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - licensing step:** The Ministry of Health report dated 2026-06-18 says
  a national verification team, the National Transplant Committee, and
  provincial and city health authorities conducted an on-site conformity
  assessment for RSUP Dr. Kariadi's bone-marrow-transplant service license.
- **Fact - assessed domains:** The official report names human resources,
  infrastructure, service governance, and quality assurance as assessment
  domains. It describes the visit as a step in the licensing process, not the
  final decision.
- **Fact - current facility listing:** The current Ministry of Health SIRS
  profile for facility `3374010` identifies RSUP Dr. Kariadi as a class A general
  hospital and lists bone marrow transplantation and cell or stem-cell therapy
  among its services. It also lists pediatric and adult hematology-oncology
  capacity.
- **Fact - earlier activity signal:** A 2025 Ministry report says the center
  could perform bone marrow transplantation for hematologic malignancies.
  Earlier Ministry material describes a transplant team and staff training and
  mentions thalassemia as a condition for which transplantation may be relevant.
  None of these sources reports a thalassemia transplant at Kariadi.

## Contradictions And Evidence Limits

- Earlier official pages describe an existing team and transplant activity,
  while the June 2026 page describes a licensing conformity assessment. The
  public sources do not identify whether this was a new license, renewal, scope
  expansion, or another administrative step.
- Field verification is not a license decision. The report provides no license
  identifier, effective date, authorization scope, decision letter, or later
  approval.
- The official search index and indexed article content were retrievable during
  this review, while a direct command-line fetch of the article returned HTTP
  404. The link is therefore unstable provenance, not a substitute for the
  formal decision document.
- The SIRS profile is a current facility listing. It does not establish
  thalassemia-specific authorization, protocol governance, service volume,
  operating status, or quality outcomes.
- No complete screened, conditioned, transplanted, engrafted, or followed
  thalassemia denominator was found. No transfusion-independence, survival,
  graft-failure, VOD, infection, GVHD, endocrine, fertility, organ, or long-term
  follow-up outcome was found for this center.
- No itemized thalassemia HSCT cost, waiting time, BPJS decision, product access,
  pharmacokinetic-monitoring capacity, complication cost, or follow-up cost was
  found.

## Cure-Program Implications

- **Hypothesis:** A government referral hospital can become a second governed
  Indonesia matched-sibling HSCT execution site only if its authorization scope,
  thalassemia protocol, complete attempted denominator, long-term outcomes, and
  delivered cost are independently documented.
- **Interpretation:** The new integration narrows the Indonesia capacity gap from
  one private pediatric two-case execution signal to an additional government
  capacity-development signal. It does not establish thalassemia execution or
  change the route ranking.
- **Open question:** What formal decision followed the June 2026 assessment, and
  does its scope include governed pediatric or adult allogeneic HSCT for
  thalassemia?
- **Affordability and access:** Government ownership and a facility listing may
  matter for future access, but neither is a payer or affordability result. The
  public sources do not provide a comparable delivered-cost estimate.
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove the capacity-development benchmark if the
  formal decision denies or defers authorization, or if the center cannot
  document the required scope and quality system. Do not promote it to an
  execution benchmark without a complete thalassemia denominator and outcomes.
  Do not promote matched-sibling HSCT beyond `benchmark_only` without acceptable
  long-term safety, durable transfusion independence, and itemized delivered
  cost.
- **Next decisive action:** A regulatory owner should obtain the public-safe
  license decision, identifier, effective date, and scope. A transplant-program
  and health-economics owner should then obtain thalassemia-specific screened,
  conditioned, transplanted, and followed denominators, safety and durability
  outcomes, and itemized payer-relevant cost. No patient routing follows.
