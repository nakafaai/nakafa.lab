# Research Status: 2026-08-05

Audience: general research planning
Status: source-backed preclinical material-recovery decision, not treatment advice

## Question

Does the verified apheresis-waste preprint record close the beta-thalassemia
cell-source gap enough to enter Nakafa Lab's current affordable HbF and
globin-chain-balance quote?

## Decision

`hold_apheresis_waste_cd34_hspcs_at_preclinical_hemoglobinopathy_material_recovery_benchmark_only`

The verified abstract supports recovery feasibility across an unspecified
hemoglobinopathy specimen set. It does not verify a beta-thalassemia material
source and does not enter the current quote. This replaces the morning
promotion label after a source-depth audit. It does not promote a therapy, a
cure, a clinical manufacturing route, or an affordable patient route. All
therapeutic and curative-route rankings remain unchanged.

Confidence: moderate for practical recovery feasibility; very low for
beta-thalassemia-specific reproducibility, affordability, and Indonesia access.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - source and design:** The official bioRxiv API reports version 1, DOI
  `10.64898/2026.08.02.742313`, posted on 2026-08-04 and not peer reviewed. The
  abstract describes a 14-month operational preclinical series that recovered,
  purified, and banked CD34-positive HSPCs from residual clinical apheresis
  product and mobilized peripheral blood, plus a separate microfluidic pilot
  and an NBSGW mouse-engraftment test.
- **Fact - population:** The abstract frames the work around sickle cell
  disease and beta-thalassemia. It does not report total patients, independent
  donors, diagnosis-specific counts, beta-thalassemia donor count, genotype,
  phenotype, or whether repeated specimens came from the same donor.
- **Fact - recovery:** Immunomagnetic selection recovered a median 4.71 million
  CD34-positive cells from 1-2 mL of apheresis product waste. The abstract
  reports a comparable 6.0 million cells from 10-40 times more peripheral-blood
  waste and similar purity across sources and diagnoses.
- **Fact - process signal:** The concentrated apheresis waste did not require
  the density-gradient steps used for peripheral-blood waste, approximately
  halving processing time. Recovered cells engrafted NBSGW mice.
- **Fact - microfluidic pilot:** A separate pilot covered two patients and 11
  specimens and recovered 2.17 million CD34-positive cells per specimen at
  more than 90% viability and purity.
- **Fact - declared interests and funding metadata:** The bioRxiv article page
  says the authors declared no competing interest and lists the Foundation for
  the National Institutes of Health and the United States Department of Defense
  under funder information. The official API payload instead records the
  `funder` field as `NA`, so funding metadata are internally inconsistent.

## Contradictions And Limits

- The title and conclusion frame a patient-derived beta-hemoglobinopathy
  biobank, but the abstract does not identify any beta-thalassemia donor or
  diagnosis-specific denominator. "Similar across diagnoses" cannot resolve
  which diagnoses contributed material or how many independent donors existed.
- The official page and API available to this audit exposed the abstract and
  metadata. No source-linked donor manifest, full results table, supplement, or
  donor-level dataset was verified. No full-text-only assertion is used here.
- Mouse engraftment supports preserved repopulating capacity in that model. It
  does not establish erythroid differentiation, HbF, F-cells, total hemoglobin,
  globin-chain balance, free alpha-globin, hemolysis, clinical manufacturing,
  or treatment benefit.
- More than 90% viability and purity describes the small microfluidic pilot. It
  is not a validated release threshold for every source or process.
- Smaller input volume and shorter processing are operational signals. The
  source reports no consumable, labor, storage, quality-control, shipping,
  failure, or cost-per-qualified-aliquot result.
- Existing apheresis infrastructure is a prerequisite. No Indonesia-specific
  governance, center capacity, biobank, shipment, or access route is reported.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** A qualified center with existing apheresis infrastructure,
  compatible consent, ethics approval, and research-only chain of custody may
  be able to convert otherwise discarded beta-thalassemia product waste into a
  lower-cost, multi-donor primary-cell model for Nakafa Lab's assay funnel.
- **Interpretation:** The abstract supports a hemoglobinopathy
  material-recovery benchmark only. It cannot establish a beta-thalassemia
  material source or enter the current quote until donor provenance is
  auditable. It cannot change a candidate ranking until erythroid function,
  safety, and cost are independently reproduced.
- **Open question:** Can the route recover usable HSPCs from at least three
  genetically distinct beta-thalassemia donors while preserving erythroid and
  globin endpoints and lowering actual cost per qualified aliquot versus the
  current source?
- **Affordability and access:** Waste recovery could reduce acquisition and
  processing burden, but no measured saving exists. Ethics, consent,
  de-identification, biosafety, chain of custody, apheresis infrastructure,
  quality control, and storage remain required inputs.

## Falsification Criterion

Do not move the benchmark into a quote or experiment until a source package
verifies the beta-thalassemia donor and sample denominator. Remove or
deprioritize the route if:

- beta-thalassemia donor provenance cannot be confirmed;
- research-grade yield, purity, viability, and erythroid function are not
  reproducible across at least three genetically distinct donors;
- recovered cells materially underperform the comparator on colony formation,
  erythroid maturation, enucleation, HbF, F-cells, total hemoglobin,
  globin-chain balance, free alpha-globin, viability, or hemolysis; or
- actual cost per qualified aliquot is not lower than the current source.

## Next Decisive Action

The research evidence owner should obtain and archive a source-linked full
manuscript or supplement, a de-identified diagnosis and donor manifest, the
independent-patient and specimen denominators, and diagnosis-stratified results.
Approved author clarification can substitute only if it is recorded as direct
correspondence and kept separate from peer-reviewed evidence. If that package
confirms beta-thalassemia material, a lab-operations owner can price an optional
paired feasibility line covering donor provenance, input volume, yield, purity,
viability, colony formation, erythroid maturation and enucleation, HbF and
F-cells, total hemoglobin, globin-chain balance, free alpha-globin, hemolysis,
failed-run denominator, and actual staff, consumable, storage, quality-control,
and failure cost. No outreach, patient action, or sample routing is authorized
by this decision.
