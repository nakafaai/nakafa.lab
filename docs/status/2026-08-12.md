# Research Status: 2026-08-12

Audience: general research planning
Status: beti-cel process-burden decision, not treatment advice

## Question

Do denominator-aligned clinical-trial and real-world beti-cel collection data
close the collection, manufacturing, and affordability gaps enough to promote
beti-cel beyond a durable long-term transfusion-independence benchmark for
transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia?

## Decision

`hold_beti_cel_at_durable_long_term_transfusion_independence_benchmark_only_after_process_attrition_quantification`

The new synthesis closes part of yesterday's denominator gap. It quantifies
repeat collection, collection failure, screening attrition, release-testing
failure, time to infusion, admission burden, and early commercial safety in
three distinct beti-cel settings. It does not establish an affordable route.
No source provides a complete commercial referral-to-infusion denominator,
final failed-lot denominator, itemized delivered cost, Indonesia delivery
path, or comparative process data against exa-cel and matched-sibling HSCT.
Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## What Changed

The August 11 decision identified attempted collection, repeat collection,
failed lot, release time, admission, complication, fertility, and cost as the
next implementation evidence gap. The repository already contained a compact
PubMed record for a German real-world beti-cel study, but had not integrated its
screening-to-treatment denominator or full text. A targeted gap search also
found a pooled 66-person clinical-trial mobilization analysis, the corresponding
FDA intent-to-treat accounting, and a separate United States commercial-center
abstract.

Together, these sources change the process evidence from an unquantified label
requirement to an observed burden. They also show why treated-only efficacy
denominators cannot be used as delivery-throughput denominators.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - parent-trial process denominator:** DOI
  `10.1016/j.jtct.2023.12.305` reports that 66 participants initiated
  mobilization across four beti-cel parent studies. Fifty-two, 78.8%, reached
  the collection target after one mobilization cycle and 13, 19.7%, after two.
  One participant who received granulocyte colony-stimulating factor without
  plerixafor had unsuccessful mobilization and withdrew before infusion.
- **Fact - parent-trial burden and attrition:** Fifty-one of 66 participants,
  77.3%, had at least one adverse event attributed to mobilization or apheresis.
  One participant had serious thrombocytopenia and hypokalemia. Eight of 18
  splenectomized participants required two cycles, compared with 5 of 48
  nonsplenectomized participants. The FDA advisory presentation accounts for
  all three people in the 66-person parent-study intent-to-treat population who
  were not treated: inadequate mobilization, pregnancy, and withdrawal of
  consent. This reconciles that intent-to-treat denominator with 63 treated
  participants without implying three manufacturing failures.
- **Fact - peer-reviewed German real-world screen:** DOI
  `10.1182/bloodadvances.2024014104`, PMID `39418614`, reports 15 people
  evaluated for beti-cel eligibility. Four withdrew by choice, two were excluded
  for severe hepatic siderosis, one had apheresis collection failure, and eight
  received beti-cel. Seven of the eight treated participants required one
  collection cycle. One required a second cycle after CD34-positive-cell loss
  during manufacturing.
- **Fact - German time and care burden:** In the eight treated participants,
  median consent-to-infusion time was 531 days, range 338 to 585, with a
  temporary marketing suspension contributing to the delay. Median
  apheresis-to-infusion time was 113 days, range 88 to 298, and the paper
  attributes about 90 days to manufacture. Discharge occurred a median 31.5
  days after infusion, range 24 to 67. The authors report guaranteed insurance
  coverage and costs exceeding allogeneic HSCT, but provide no itemized or
  payer amount.
- **Fact - German efficacy and safety boundary:** All eight treated
  participants achieved and maintained transfusion independence at a median
  541.5 days of follow-up, range 365 to 734. Five had post-treatment gonadal
  dysfunction findings, although baseline iron-related dysfunction was not
  fully excluded. Two could not resume usual work or activities because of
  fatigue or depression and anxiety. One developed unexplained polycythemia.
- **Fact - United States commercial-center replication:** DOI
  `10.1016/j.jtct.2025.01.386` reports ten people who underwent commercial
  beti-cel collection at one center. Eight required one collection cycle and
  two required a second cycle after out-of-specification product-release
  results. Nine had been infused and followed for at least three months. All
  nine were alive, engrafted, and transfusion-independent at median 8.9 months,
  but three developed VOD and seven had clinically significant bleeding during
  prolonged thrombocytopenia. One had subarachnoid and severe mucocutaneous
  bleeding in the setting of HLA class I alloimmunization and platelet
  refractoriness. The abstract states that bleeding and organ dysfunction
  resolved.

## Contradictions And Limits

- Reaching the collection target is not the same as receiving a released drug
  product. The pooled trial abstract reports 65 of 66 reaching target, while
  the FDA record shows that pregnancy and consent withdrawal also separated
  the 66-person intent-to-treat group from the 63 treated participants.
- The German sequence reports eight treated among 15 screened. This is a
  process-throughput observation, not a 53% efficacy estimate. The excluded and
  withdrawing participants were not exchangeable with the treated cohort.
- The parent-trial and United States commercial process sources are conference
  abstracts, not complete peer-reviewed implementation datasets. The German
  study is peer-reviewed but small, selected, restricted to non-beta-zero/
  beta-zero genotypes, fully insured, and conducted during a historical
  European licensing period.
- The United States abstract does not explain why the tenth collected person
  was not yet included among the nine infused people. It therefore cannot
  supply a final manufacturing-failure or treatment-throughput denominator.
- None of the sources reports a complete commercial referral denominator,
  insurance denials, released and discarded lots, bridge transfusions,
  activity-based cost, payer payment, Indonesia authorization, local
  manufacturing, or qualified-center capacity.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Hypothesis:** Beti-cel can become a realistic affordable route only if a
  transferable process reduces screening-to-infusion attrition, repeat
  collection, out-of-specification release, conditioning toxicity, admission
  time, and total delivered cost while preserving the durable efficacy already
  observed in treated participants.
- **Interpretation:** Keep beti-cel at a durable long-term
  transfusion-independence benchmark. The process evidence is now strong enough
  to replace a generic collection-gap statement with measured burdens, but it
  argues against affordability promotion.
- **Open question:** Across all people referred for commercial beti-cel, what
  proportions reach screening, authorization, collection, released product,
  infusion, and durable transfusion independence, and what activity and payer
  cost accumulates at each step?
- **Confidence:** High that repeat collection and pre-infusion attrition occur.
  Moderate that the observed frequencies approximate delivery at experienced
  European and United States centers. Low for broader commercial throughput,
  comparative cost, transferability, and Indonesia access.
- **Affordability and access:** Repeat mobilization can add procedures and time;
  out-of-specification release can require recollection; individualized
  manufacture takes months; and busulfan conditioning adds admission,
  fertility, VOD, bleeding, and surveillance burdens. No itemized cost or
  Indonesia route was reported. Operational burden is not itself a monetary
  affordability estimate.

## Falsification Criterion

Deprioritize beti-cel as an affordable-route candidate if a complete
referral-to-infusion implementation cohort shows persistent collection or
release failure, prolonged throughput, unacceptable conditioning or bleeding
burden, or no credible activity-based total-cost and capacity advantage over
exa-cel while approaching the matched-sibling HSCT benchmark. Re-rank
immediately for confirmed insertional oncogenesis, vector-derived
replication-competent lentivirus, material loss of transfusion independence, or
a nonrecoverable manufacturing-failure pattern.

## Next Decisive Action

Obtain one owner-verified commercial implementation dataset that starts with
all referrals, not only treated participants. It should include reasons for
screen failure and withdrawal, authorization time and denial, mobilization and
apheresis cycles, released, out-of-specification and discarded lots,
recollection, collection-to-release time, bridge care, busulfan exposure,
admission and intensive-care days, VOD, bleeding, fertility preservation,
readmission, surveillance, and itemized payer-relevant cost. Compare identical
fields with exa-cel and matched-sibling HSCT. No patient action, referral, or
sample routing is authorized.
