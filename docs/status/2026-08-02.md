# Daily Research Journal: 2026-08-02

Audience: founder / researcher
Status: Indonesia pediatric matched-sibling HSCT gains a two-case execution
benchmark; the route remains benchmark only

## Question And Decision

**Question:** Does the current BPOM BUSULFEX record close the Indonesia
conditioning-access gap enough to promote matched-sibling HSCT beyond a
benchmark-only route?

**Decision:**
`promote_busulfex_to_indonesia_registered_conditioning_product_benchmark_only`.

This is a component-level regulatory finding, not a route, affordability,
availability, or treatment claim. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.
The evidence is newly integrated into the repo, not a new 2026 approval.

**Confidence:** High that a current public BPOM record exists for BUSULFEX.
Low that the record translates into a thalassemia indication, current hospital
stock, procurement, monitoring capacity, payer coverage, or affordable route
delivery.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - public registration:** The August 2 CekBPOM product-name query
  returned BUSULFEX registration `DKI1905000743A1` with status `Berlaku`, a
  product date of 2024-03-01, expiry of 2029-03-01, and Otsuka Indonesia as the
  named manufacturer entry.
- **Fact - packages:** The public search returned one registration under two
  package rows: a single 10 mL vial and a bulk package containing eight
  single-vial boxes. Package rows are not stock or purchase evidence.
- **Fact - product identity:** BPOM-approved product information identifies
  intravenous busulfan 6 mg/mL, German manufacture, and import and repacking by
  PT Otsuka Indonesia. The document was approved on 2024-02-27.
- **Fact - indication scope:** The approved product information describes
  conditioning before allogeneic transplantation for chronic myelogenous
  leukemia. It does not state a thalassemia indication.
- **Fact - infrastructure and safety:** The approved information requires
  experienced transplant supervision, adequate diagnostic and treatment
  facilities, pharmacokinetic monitoring, and intensive support for profound
  myelosuppression and other serious toxicities.

## Contradictions And Evidence Limits

- BUSULFEX returned two package rows by product name, while `busulfan` returned
  zero by both product-name and ingredient fields. An ingredient-only search
  would have produced a false public-registry gap for this product.
- The product information approval date is 2024-02-27, while CekBPOM reports a
  product date of 2024-03-01. These are kept as separate source fields, not
  forced into one regulatory event date.
- `Berlaku` and a 2029 expiry establish the current public-record status, not
  hospital stock, procurement, distribution, affordability, or payer coverage.
- The product's registered transplant indication is not thalassemia-specific.
  The record cannot be used to infer a thalassemia protocol, suitability, or
  access route.
- The source does not price the product, pharmacokinetic assay, transplant
  staffing, supportive care, toxicity management, hospitalization, or
  follow-up.

## Cure-Program Implications

- **Hypothesis:** A governed Indonesian transplant program can include a
  BPOM-registered intravenous busulfan input only if a qualified center can
  verify institutional availability, thalassemia-specific protocol governance,
  pharmacokinetic monitoring, supportive-care capacity, and itemized total
  delivered cost.
- **Interpretation:** The regulatory identity gap for one conditioning input is
  narrower than the prior generic Indonesia-access statement implied. The
  route-level safety, capacity, donor, outcome, and affordability gaps remain.
- **Open question:** Can one Indonesian transplant center verify all required
  capabilities without exceeding the program's delivered-cost threshold?
- **Affordability and access:** Registration and an Indonesian importer and
  repacker are useful access signals. They are not availability or
  affordability evidence. No price, stock, procurement lead time, hospital
  formulary, monitoring cost, or payer decision was found.
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove even the component-level access bridge if
  qualified center and pharmacy verification cannot confirm institutional
  availability and pharmacokinetic monitoring, or if the itemized route cost
  exceeds the program threshold. Do not promote matched-sibling HSCT beyond
  `benchmark_only` without independent modern outcome, safety, center-capacity,
  and cost evidence.
- **Next decisive action:** A transplant-program and health-economics owner
  should produce one public-safe Indonesia center-capacity and itemized-cost
  dataset covering product availability, pharmacokinetic monitoring,
  transplant staffing, supportive care, complication management, and
  follow-up. No patient routing or treatment recommendation follows.

## Late Indonesia Route Integration

**Question:** Do the Jakarta two-case report, current center page, and hospital
cost range close enough of the Indonesia delivery gap to promote
matched-sibling HSCT beyond a benchmark-only route?

**Decision:**
`promote_tzu_chi_pediatric_msd_hsct_to_indonesia_execution_benchmark_only`.

This promotes documented local execution at one pediatric center, not routine
availability, affordability, suitability, or a treatment recommendation.
Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

- **Fact - mechanism and scope:** APBMT abstract `A-198` is a two-person case
  report of pediatric beta-thalassemia major treated with allogeneic HSCT at
  Tzu Chi Hospital Jakarta. Both donors were HLA-matched brothers. The report
  does not state `HBB` genotype or TDT classification under current criteria.
- **Fact - conditioning and endpoint:** The abstract reports `Bu4Cy2+ATG` for
  one case and `Bu4Cy4+ATG` for the other. Neutrophil engraftment occurred on
  days 11 and 12, platelet engraftment on days 43 and 25, and both had 100%
  donor chimerism at day 28.
- **Fact - safety:** Both cases developed veno-occlusive disease despite
  ursodeoxycholic-acid prophylaxis and received defibrotide plus
  low-molecular-weight heparin. Two events in two selected cases are an
  important center-level safety signal, not a stable incidence estimate.
- **Evidence tier:** Primary conference case report with two selected cases,
  no comparator, no complete attempted denominator, and no reported
  transfusion-independence, mortality, graft-failure, infection, GVHD,
  fertility, endocrine, organ, quality-of-life, or cost outcome beyond the
  short engraftment window.
- **Resolved source overlap:** The 2023 APBMT abstract describes the first case
  while still in pre-HSCT screening. The 2024 abstract reports two transplanted
  cases. These are sequential reports with at least one overlapping case, not
  evidence for three independent transplants.
- **Fact - current center signal:** The hospital's current facility page lists
  a pediatric blood-stem-cell transplant unit with trained specialist staff,
  apheresis equipment, sterile recovery rooms, and diagnostic support. This is
  a current facility claim, not a current case-volume, outcome, or access
  dataset.
- **Affordability and access:** A September 2025 hospital article gives a
  general Indonesian HSCT estimate of Rp800 million to Rp2 billion. It is not
  itemized, thalassemia-specific, a center quote, or a payer decision. It is not
  directly comparable with the USD 8,000-10,000 mean reported by the 2026 LMIC
  cohort because scope, setting, included services, and follow-up are not
  aligned.
- **Hypothesis:** A governed Indonesian pediatric matched-sibling HSCT program
  can reproduce durable transfusion independence with acceptable toxicity only
  if its complete attempted denominator, pharmacokinetic monitoring, long-term
  outcomes, and delivered cost are measured prospectively.
- **Interpretation:** The prior generic local-delivery gap is now narrowed to a
  two-case execution benchmark. The two VOD events and missing denominator,
  durability, capacity, and cost evidence block route promotion.
- **Open question:** Since the first 2023 case, how many children have been
  screened, conditioned, transplanted, engrafted, and followed, and what are
  their complete one-, two-, and five-year outcomes and costs?
- **Falsification criterion:** Remove the local execution benchmark if the
  center cannot confirm an ongoing governed service or a complete attempted
  denominator. Remove the leading affordable-route label if independent modern
  follow-up shows unacceptable death, graft failure, VOD, infection, GVHD,
  endocrine or fertility harm, loss of transfusion independence, or an
  itemized Indonesia cost above the program threshold.
- **Next decisive action:** A transplant-program owner should obtain one
  public-safe center dataset with screened, conditioned, transplanted, and
  followed denominators; phenotype, genotype, donor and regimen scope;
  busulfan pharmacokinetic monitoring; engraftment, transfusion, survival,
  VOD, infection, GVHD, organ, endocrine, and fertility outcomes; and follow-up
  dates. A health-economics owner should reconcile that dataset with an
  itemized hospital and payer cost model. No patient routing follows.
