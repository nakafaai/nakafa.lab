# Research Status: 2026-08-16

Audience: general research planning
Status: HLA gene-therapy process predictor held after conflicting cohorts

## Question

Does the 249-patient Thai multicenter pediatric TDT HSCT cohort, together with
official Universal Coverage Scheme evidence, close the Southeast Asian outcome
and payer gap enough to promote matched-donor HSCT beyond benchmark-only status?

## Decision

`promote_thailand_pediatric_hsct_to_southeast_asian_multicenter_outcome_and_payer_path_benchmark_only`

Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

## What Changed

The morning PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov queries returned no newly posted
curative evidence. The final PubMed rerun is recorded below. A targeted
Southeast Asian HSCT and payer-gap search surfaced a previously unintegrated
five-center Thai cohort and official payer records.

The cohort adds multi-center delivery, era-stratified outcomes,
pharmacokinetic-monitoring context, and late-effect reporting. Thai National
Health Security Office records separately establish that stem-cell transplant
for thalassemia entered the Universal Coverage Scheme benefit package in 2020
and document one reimbursed pediatric matched-sibling procedure.

The combination closes the existence of a regional payer mechanism, not current
benefit adequacy or affordability. The route ranking does not change.

## Direct Evidence

- **Fact - design:** PMID `41821157`, PMCID `PMC13076047`, and DOI
  `10.3345/cep.2025.02173` report a retrospective cohort from five Thai centers.
  It includes 249 people younger than 20 years who underwent 266 related or
  unrelated HSCT procedures from 1988 through 2016, with follow-up through 2021.
- **Fact - outcome:** Median follow-up was 102 months. The study's
  procedure-based five-year overall survival and event-free survival estimates
  were 91.3% and 81.0%. For 86 procedures in 2010-2016, the estimates were 93.0%
  and 87.2%.
- **Fact - donor scope:** Of 266 procedures, 191 used HLA-matched related
  donors, 59 matched unrelated donors, nine mismatched related donors, and seven
  mismatched unrelated donors. The restricted 2000-2016 matched-donor
  comparison included 135 related and 59 unrelated procedures.
- **Fact - observed burden:** The report gives 31 thalassemia recurrences and 24
  deaths, including 16 infection deaths. It reports 33 VOD events, 71 acute
  graft-versus-host disease events, and 39 chronic graft-versus-host disease
  events across all procedures.
- **Fact - late effects:** Among 211 people who remained thalassemia-free for
  more than two years, 48 had at least one reported late complication. The
  median follow-up in this survivor group was 10.5 years.
- **Fact - payer path:** The official NHSO 2022 annual report records that
  stem-cell transplant for thalassemia entered the Universal Coverage Scheme
  benefit package in 2020. An official NHSO account documents reimbursement of
  transplant and related medical fees for one pediatric matched-sibling
  procedure.

## Contradictions And Limits

- Seventeen people underwent a second HSCT. The survival, recurrence, toxicity,
  and mortality tables use 266 procedures rather than 249 unique people, so the
  estimates are not a clean first-transplant patient denominator.
- The article reports 28 endocrine cases in prose while Table 5 reports 29.
- Table 4 prints an event-free survival confidence interval of `3.08-3.13`
  around an RIC hazard ratio of `1.09`. That interval cannot contain the point
  estimate.
- The matched-related and matched-unrelated survival differences were not
  statistically significant, but the study was not an equivalence trial.
  Matched-unrelated procedures had more VOD and acute graft-versus-host disease
  in the restricted comparison.
- From 2010 all centers used intravenous rather than oral busulfan, and the
  article says most patients had pharmacokinetic monitoring without reporting a
  denominator. Era, donor mix, conditioning, and supportive care changed
  together, so no causal busulfan or regimen advantage follows.
- Enrollment ended in 2016. Neither the cohort nor the payer sources provide a
  current 2026 benefit specification, complete utilization, capacity, waiting
  time, benefit cap, hospital payment, family cost, itemized delivery cost, or
  Indonesia transfer path.

## Cure-Program Read

- **Mechanism and scope:** Conditioning followed by allogeneic donor HSPC
  infusion replaces ineffective recipient erythropoiesis. The observed scope is
  pediatric related and unrelated HSCT across five Thai centers, not a current
  protocol or pure matched-sibling cohort.
- **Evidence tier:** Direct human outcome evidence from a retrospective,
  nonrandomized multicenter cohort, paired with official historical payer
  records.
- **Safety risk:** Infection accounted for 16 of 24 reported deaths. The study
  also reports recurrence, VOD, acute and chronic graft-versus-host disease,
  endocrine disorders, and other late effects.
- **Hypothesis:** A standardized Southeast Asian matched-sibling program with
  complete pharmacokinetic, safety, late-effect, payer, and cost capture can
  reproduce the Thai 2010-2016 event-free survival while lowering infection,
  VOD, graft-versus-host disease, and recurrence burden and retaining a
  delivered-cost advantage over individualized autologous gene-cell therapy.
- **Interpretation:** Promote the Thai evidence to a Southeast Asian
  multicenter outcome-and-payer-path benchmark only. It does not establish a
  current matched-sibling outcome rate, donor equivalence, causal regimen
  benefit, delivered affordability, Indonesia access, or individual
  suitability.
- **Open question:** What are the current Thai screening-to-HSCT denominator,
  first-versus-repeat procedure outcomes, benefit criteria, payer payment,
  family cost, center capacity, waiting time, and late-effect outcomes under a
  standardized regimen?
- **Confidence:** High that the sources establish multicenter historical
  execution and one public payer mechanism. Moderate for the procedure-based
  outcome estimates. Low for unique-patient causal comparisons, current access,
  affordability, Indonesia transfer, or suitability.
- **Affordability and access:** The payer evidence closes mechanism existence,
  not adequacy. It supplies no itemized cohort cost or aligned gene-cell
  comparator and cannot be transferred to Indonesia without local policy,
  capacity, and cost evidence.

## Falsification Criterion

Remove the benchmark if unique-patient reanalysis materially lowers the
reported five-year outcomes or official records show the payer path was not
implemented. Do not promote a route until a current, consecutive cohort reports
complete screening-to-follow-up, severe-toxicity, late-effect, payer, and
itemized-cost denominators and meets the existing durability and lifetime
delivered-cost thresholds.

## Next Decisive Action

Obtain the 2026 Thai benefit specification and utilization denominator. Then
request center-stratified unique-patient data that count first and repeat HSCT
separately and align donor, regimen, pharmacokinetic monitoring, infection,
graft failure, mortality, late effects, payer payment, and family cost. Compare
identical fields with the Malaysian, LMIC, and Indonesia benchmarks. No patient
action, referral, or sample routing follows.

## Final Integration: HLA Class I Gene-Therapy Process Risk

### Question

Does HLA class I alloimmunization independently predict delayed platelet
engraftment, transfusion burden, and bleeding after busulfan-conditioned
autologous gene therapy for TDT strongly enough to become a validated process
risk marker?

### Decision

`hold_hla_class_i_alloimmunization_at_plausible_gene_therapy_process_risk_signal_only`

Beti-cel and exa-cel retain their existing curative-benchmark and
process-burden holds. Matched-sibling HSCT remains
`leading_affordable_curative_route_benchmark_only`.

### Direct Evidence

- **Fact - positive cohort:** PMID `40089998`, PMCID `PMC12824613`, and DOI
  `10.1182/blood.2024027034` report a retrospective CHOP cohort of 18 people
  with TDT who received gene-modified autologous HCT from 2015 through 2024.
  Thirteen had panel-reactive antibody testing. Four were `PRAhigh`, defined as
  reactivity to at least 80% of potential platelet donors, and nine were
  `PRAlow`.
- **Fact - positive association:** The CHOP report gives median platelet
  engraftment of 75 versus 44 days, 59 versus 24 platelet transfusions, and 18
  versus 7 red-cell transfusions for the four `PRAhigh` participants versus six
  `PRAlow` participants without VOD. Statistical significance at `p=0.02` for
  each outcome followed exclusion of the one splenectomized `PRAhigh`
  participant. One participant with 100% PRA had subarachnoid and severe
  mucocutaneous bleeding.
- **Fact - negative cohort:** A separate Sarah Cannon conference abstract, DOI
  `10.1016/j.jtct.2025.12.037`, reports 24 TDT gene-therapy recipients, 13 with
  and 11 without HLA class I antibodies. Median platelet engraftment was 38.5
  versus 35.5 days, `p=0.59`, and median platelet transfusions were 14 versus
  5.5, `p=0.16`. Fifteen of 24 TDT participants had grade 2 to 4 bleeding, but
  bleeding did not differ significantly by HLA antibody status. One
  antibody-positive TDT participant had intracranial hemorrhage.
- **Fact - late-indexed source:** PMID `42603589` and DOI
  `10.1016/j.jtct.2026.08.027` identify a separate August 15 journal article
  with the same title and overlapping Sarah Cannon authors. PubMed supplies no
  abstract, the publisher exposes metadata but not the article text, and
  Crossref provides no formal relation to the conference DOI. Results from the
  conference abstract are therefore not transferred to the new article.

### Contradiction And Limits

- CHOP tested some earlier participants only after platelet refractoriness or
  delayed engraftment appeared. Only 13 of 18 gene-therapy recipients were
  tested, three `PRAlow` participants with VOD were analyzed separately, and
  significance followed exclusion of the one splenectomized `PRAhigh`
  participant. The final comparison is too small and selected for causal
  inference.
- The Sarah Cannon abstract used antibody-positive versus antibody-negative
  groups after screening within seven days before busulfan. CHOP used a severe
  `PRAhigh` threshold of at least 80%. The studies therefore do not test the
  same exposure contrast.
- Both sources mix clinical-trial and commercial gene therapies and do not
  provide a product-stratified TDT result. CHOP included beti-cel and one
  exa-cel recipient among the tested participants; the Sarah Cannon abstract
  does not report its product split or genotype distribution.
- CHOP authors disclose bluebird bio and Vertex research or consulting
  relationships. The late-indexed article's PubMed record discloses Vertex and
  BEAM consulting relationships among other conflicts. These disclosures do
  not invalidate the data but reinforce the need for independent replication.

### Cure-Program Read

- **Mechanism and phenotype scope:** Repeated transfusion can induce HLA class
  I antibodies that bind transfused platelets and promote splenic clearance.
  The observed scope is repeatedly transfused TDT recipients undergoing
  busulfan-conditioned autologous gene therapy. Neither study supports a
  genotype-specific effect.
- **Evidence tier:** Conflicting retrospective single-center human evidence,
  with one peer-reviewed brief report and one conference abstract. The new
  journal article is title- and metadata-level evidence until its methods and
  results are available.
- **Safety risk:** Prolonged thrombocytopenia, high platelet-transfusion burden,
  clinically significant bleeding, and intracranial or subarachnoid hemorrhage
  remain material process risks. The reviewed evidence does not establish HLA
  antibodies as their independent cause.
- **Hypothesis:** Severe pre-busulfan HLA class I alloimmunization increases
  platelet refractoriness and bleeding burden after gene-modified HCT when
  product, VOD, splenic status, busulfan exposure, CD34-positive dose, and
  transfusion practice are aligned.
- **Interpretation:** Hold HLA class I alloimmunization as a plausible process
  risk signal only, not a validated causal predictor or product-specific safety
  marker. Retain bleeding and transfusion support as route-level implementation
  burdens regardless of antibody status.
- **Open question:** Does severe, prospectively measured PRA predict a
  clinically material delay or transfusion burden within each gene-therapy
  product after prespecified adjustment for VOD, splenic status, conditioning,
  cell dose, and center practice?
- **Affordability and access:** Neither cohort reports HLA-laboratory,
  HLA-selected platelet, admission, complication, or total delivered costs.
  No affordability, payer, capacity, Indonesia access, or individual
  suitability conclusion follows.

### Falsification Criterion

Deprioritize HLA class I as an independent process predictor if a consecutive,
product-stratified TDT cohort with universal pre-busulfan testing, a prespecified
severe-PRA threshold, aligned engraftment and bleeding definitions, and adjusted
analysis excludes a clinically material association. Promote it only if that
design independently reproduces delayed engraftment or transfusion and bleeding
burden without selection by an already observed complication.

### Next Decisive Action

A gene-cell therapy center, transfusion-medicine service, and HLA laboratory
should obtain the full PMID `42603589` report and build a pooled, de-duplicated
participant table. It should align product, genotype, baseline transfusion and
alloimmunization history, pre-busulfan PRA method and severity, splenic status,
busulfan exposure, CD34-positive dose, VOD, platelet-support practice,
engraftment, bleeding grade, length of stay, and itemized support cost. No
patient action or treatment recommendation follows.
