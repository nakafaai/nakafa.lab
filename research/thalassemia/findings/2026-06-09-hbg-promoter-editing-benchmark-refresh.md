# Finding: HBG Promoter Editing Benchmark Refresh

Date checked: 2026-06-09
Last evidence update: 2026-08-08
Evidence label: benchmark refresh and novelty gate, not treatment advice

## Direct Answer

The June 9 evidence refresh tightens the same conclusion started by `CS-101`:
HBG promoter editing and HbF-mediated transfusion independence are already
clinical benchmark territory. Nakafa Lab should not claim that HbF rescue,
HBG1/HBG2 promoter editing, or HPFH-like restoration is new by itself.

An August 6 NEJM correction preserves reni-cel as an early clinical mechanism
benchmark but materially tightens its engraftment and safety record. It does not
support route, durability, safety, access, or affordability promotion.

The useful open lane remains narrower:

> Can a cheaper, lower-infrastructure, non-editing candidate pass HbF or `HBG`,
> alpha-globin/autophagy, maturation, viability, and hemolysis gates before any
> clinical claim is made?

## August 8 Correction Audit

**Question:** Does the formal correction to the reni-cel phase 1-2 report leave
it reliable enough for promotion beyond an early clinical HBG-promoter-editing
benchmark?

**Decision:**
`hold_reni_cel_at_corrected_early_clinical_hbg_promoter_editing_benchmark_only`.

- **Fact - mechanism and scope:** The corrected primary report describes
  autologous CD34-positive HSPCs edited with AsCas12a at the BCL11A binding
  sites in the `HBG1` and `HBG2` promoters to reactivate HbF. The
  nonprespecified analysis covered nine adults with TDT: three beta-zero/
  beta-zero, one beta-plus/beta-plus with homozygous IVS-I-110 G-to-A, four
  beta-zero/beta-plus, and one HbE/beta-zero. The median observed age was 20
  years, with a range of 18 to 29 years.
- **Fact - design and endpoint:** This was a sponsor-funded, multicenter,
  open-label, single-group phase 1-2 study terminated early after a development
  priority change. Eleven participants completed mobilization and apheresis;
  nine were treated. One of the other two withdrew and one became ineligible
  for conditioning after a Crohn disease diagnosis. No manufacturing failure
  was reported for those two exclusions. All nine treated participants had
  neutrophil engraftment by day 42. The correction removes platelet engraftment
  from that claim; the paper's Table 1 reports platelet engraftment from day 23
  through day 49.
- **Fact - efficacy boundary:** All nine participants were transfusion-free at
  their last observed visit, for a median 17.0 months and a range of 3.1 to
  22.7 months. Only six had enough follow-up to be evaluated for at least 12
  months of transfusion independence. This is early clinical activity, not a
  complete durable-cure denominator.
- **Fact - corrected safety denominator:** The correction replaces narrative
  rates of 7%, 6%, and 3% with decreased platelet count in 5 of 9 participants
  across 13 events, decreased neutrophil count in 5 of 9 across 12 events, and
  stomatitis in 6 of 9 across 6 events. The report records 69 grade 3 or 4
  events in nine participants and six serious events in four participants.
  No death or malignancy was reported by the data cutoff, but follow-up was too
  short to resolve late genomic, marrow, organ, endocrine, or fertility risk.
- **Fact - mechanism correction:** The correction changes one Discussion
  reference from a GATA1 binding site to the BCL11A binding site, aligning the
  narrative with the Methods and product design.
- **Conflict:** The current public `NCT05444894` record remains active, not
  recruiting, with no posted results, while the paper states that development
  was discontinued in December 2024 and the study terminated early. The
  registry label therefore cannot establish active development. The separate
  15-year `NCT06363760` follow-up is enrolling by invitation and has no posted
  results.
- **Hypothesis:** Corrected long-term participant-level follow-up could retain
  reni-cel as a useful mechanism and durability comparator if editing,
  hematopoiesis, HbF, and transfusion independence remain stable without an
  unacceptable late safety burden.
- **Interpretation:** The correction resolves internal paper contradictions and
  strengthens denominator traceability. It also makes the early safety burden
  less favorable than the original narrative implied. The efficacy signal
  remains benchmark evidence, but the small cohort, short follow-up, stopped
  development, full busulfan route, and missing cost and access evidence block
  promotion.
- **Open question:** Across every treated TDT participant, what are the
  corrected platelet-engraftment denominator, treatment-related and
  conditioning-related event attribution, five-year transfusion status,
  editing persistence, malignancy and clonal findings, marrow function, organ,
  endocrine, fertility, manufacturing-failure, and delivered-cost outcomes?
- **Affordability and access:** The sources provide no itemized manufacturing
  cost, scaled commercial manufacturing-failure rate, delivered price,
  Indonesia authorization, qualified-center capacity, payer coverage, or total
  follow-up cost. Discontinued development prevents use as a current access
  route.
- **Falsification criterion:** Deprioritize even the early clinical benchmark
  if corrected participant-level follow-up does not sustain editing, HbF, or
  transfusion independence, or if death, malignancy, clonal expansion, marrow
  failure, organ harm, endocrine or fertility harm, or manufacturing failure
  makes the risk-benefit profile unacceptable. Do not promote it as an
  affordable route without itemized delivered cost and a reproducible access
  pathway.
- **Next decisive owner action:** The research evidence owner should reconcile
  the correction against the abstract, Results narrative, Table 1, Table 2,
  `NCT05444894`, and `NCT06363760`, then obtain a complete corrected
  participant-level long-term denominator. That package must separate product,
  busulfan, transplant, and follow-up harms before any ranking change.

## Decision Rule

Reject or downgrade a candidate claim if it only says "raises HbF",
"reactivates gamma-globin", "copies HPFH", "targets HBG", or "could reduce
transfusions" without a validation path.

Keep a candidate researchable only if it adds affordability or lower
infrastructure, non-editing validation, HbF or `HBG` readout, alpha-globin or
autophagy readout, maturation and viability checks, hemolysis stop rules, and
clinician-reviewed relevance before patient-specific claims.
